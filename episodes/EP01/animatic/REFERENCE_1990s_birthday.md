# A 1990s Mongolian apartment birthday — reference for the EP01 dressing

Research pass: 30 October 2026. Naran is fictional. This note is for dressing Altan's third birthday in a late-Soviet panel flat in a provincial aimag centre, poor-to-ordinary family, late November, about 21:00. The user is Mongolian and will correct it.

Labels: **verified** means a source below actually says it. **to verify** means it is common knowledge, the manager's brief, or a reasonable inference, and it has not been checked against a Mongolian source from the period.

## Sources

1. Regions.ru, 2026, «Жизнь по-монгольски: чем их квартиры отличаются от подмосковных» — Ulaanbaatar housing stock. Names **угсармал байр**: large-panel blocks built in the 1960s–80s to Soviet type plans, about 15% of the capital's housing. Small kitchens (often 6–7 m²), the real dining table in the living room, tea as a household habit. https://regions.ru/ekonomika/zhizn-po-mongolski-chem-ih-kvartiry-otlichajutsja-ot-podmoskovnyh
2. Russia Beyond / Gateway to Russia photo essays on the ordinary Soviet flat (2020–2023): wall carpet, стенка, crystal and china kept for display, carpets on the floor because a bare floor felt unfinished, tear-off calendar, birthdays held at home because a restaurant was out of reach, the table carried into the living room. https://www.rbth.com/history/332727-soviet-furniture-home and https://www.gw2ru.com/lifestyle/1737-soviet-apartment-photos and https://www.gw2ru.com/history/1588-items-soviet-apartment
3. Absatz, 2026, «Быт 90-х»: the 1990s home still had the wooden стенка, the china in the сервант, and the carpet on the wall. Euro-remont was what people with money did to escape it. https://absatz.media/video/142945-byt-90-h-razrisovannye-poduezdy-kover-na-stene-hrustal-v-servante
4. Métropolitiques, 2013, «Mongolian (Urban) Homes» — moving from a ger into an apartment did not wipe the old habits: shared sleeping, photos, carpets, the living room used as the real social room. https://metropolitiques.eu/Mongolian-urban-homes.html
5. Facts and Details, ger-life summary — a 1990s urban household spent most of its money on food; carpets, a china cabinet, a television, photographs and tea things were the possessions people actually had. The ger inventory is context for what came into the flat, not a plan of this apartment. https://factsanddetails.com/central-asia/Mongolia/sub8_2c/entry-4584.html
6. Buuz as feast food: National Geographic (Czech edition) on buuz at a home feast; academic discussion of buuz and bansh as identity food, with urban appropriation noted from the late 1990s. https://www.nationalgeographic.cz/cestovani/co-jedi-mongolsti-kocovnici/ and https://www.academia.edu/31730281/
7. This repo, `docs/research/naran-1998-environment.md` — Sukhbaatar November normals (mean daily minimum about −14°C), incandescent and fluorescent practicals, no LEDs, and the warning not to dress a provincial town from present-day Ulaanbaatar.

No 1998 photograph of a provincial Mongolian child's birthday was found in this pass. The dressing is a synthesis, not a reconstruction of one flat.

## What this pass refused

- Present-day Ulaanbaatar interiors, LED strips, plastic party shops, printed banners, balloon arches.
- A ger dressed as the apartment. The ger sources explain carpets, photos, tea and china, then stop.
- Brand logos on the television, the vodka, the sweets or the soda.
- A bright, evenly lit "set". The warmth belongs over the table. The window and the landing stay cold.
