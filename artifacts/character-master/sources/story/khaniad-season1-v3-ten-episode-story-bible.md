# ХАНИАД — THE COUGH
## SEASON 1 · V3.0 STORY REBUILD · TEN EPISODES

**Status:** proposed season architecture and scene-by-scene dramatic treatment; **NOT** finished scripts for Episodes 2–10. **Episode 1 remains based on the existing v2.1 production-directed screenplay**, subject to its own final timed animatic and the small checks flagged below.

**Format:** ten episodes × **8–10 minutes** (target ~90 minutes). Late November 1998. Fictional northern-Mongolian town of **Naran**, south of a Russia–Mongolia road corridor; invented side roads, station, ridges and facilities. 1998 is a specific production commitment, not a reason to pretend that every obscure local detail is verified.

**Voice:** adult Mongolian psychological and survival horror. Dark comedy comes out of personalities, hypocrisy and familiar relationships, not quips during a death. Mongolian spoken dialogue, Russian with Mongolian subtitles when justified; English scene/action direction for production.

**One-sentence dramatic engine:** A driver dies before he can deliver a warning. His daughter survives, a doctor carries the consequences of an impossible choice, a teenager records what adults deny, and a frightened soldier joins a family heading toward a mountain radio station before an advancing cold front makes the road impassable.

### The promise to the audience

By the end of any episode viewers should feel: (a) someone they know made a recognizable decision; (b) the next danger follows from it; (c) they learned something about the infection **through behavior**, not lecture; and (d) one human relationship is different from when the episode began.

The question isn't “How many zombies can we fit into eight minutes?” It is **“When someone is already in trouble, what will the next person do?”** The series remains frightening even in scenes where no infected person appears.

# I. CANON: WHAT WE KEEP FROM EPISODE 1

**Locked in spirit and staging:** Ulan-Ude freight yard; Irina recording the abnormal three-minute sprint; her urgent satchel; Ganaa's shabby KAMAZ, gift, and warmth; Dasha's forearm and lies; salty tea shared from one bowl (NOT a means of infection); genuine camaraderie with Hürel; café attack; bite to Ganaa's wrist; wrecked KAMAZ left at the café with Irina's satchel; another driver gets Ganaa back to town; the official “flu” announcement; Ganaa staring silently at a sheep; Altan's third birthday; Tsetsegmaa noticing the bite; the switch from father to predator on the sound of a baby crying.

**Preserve this dialogue verbatim:** “Томуу гэнэ ээ? Хүрэлийг тэр чинь хазаад... идчихсэн шүү дээ. Ямар алных нь томуу вэ...” It has the right combination of profanity, lived-in disbelief, and character. Erdene's later exhausted “...Пизда.” is also protected.

**Small practical checks, not a rewrite:**
1. Track the wrist bite hour to transformation in the party: a hand wound taking roughly 5–9 hours works within the established fictional range. Put actual arrival/incident times in production notes.
2. Establish that the fire consumes the café entrance and disables the truck but does **not** burn the satchel; firefighters cannot instantly stabilize the site because the emergency response has already fractured.
3. Clarify the identity of the baby crying in the neighboring room (a guest's infant) on the casting sheet; Altan is three and **not** that baby.
4. Dasha says “sister” in Naran; keep that statement, even if she is concealing the source of her injury. She must reach Ganaa before her believable bite clock runs out.
5. End Episode 1 on the turn. Do not add a montage that explains who survived. Episode 2 earns that reveal.

## Consequences EP1 must create (not merely reference)

- **Ganaa's bite** → an attacked birthday gathering → people transported to hospital → overloaded triage, Sükhee and others turning.
- **Altan's birthday / the mittens** → the mother and child have to survive, not vanish from the story; the gift remains a human object rather than a magical clue.
- **Irina's undelivered satchel** → no early diagnosis → later retrieval at the café → evidence of an earlier official warning.
- **Hürel's death and burned café** → an identifiable place with a debt book, charred sign and broken truck → later recovered tapes, salvageable supplies, and a moral confrontation with Ganaa's memory.
- **The seasonal-flu broadcast** → citizens are given bad information → hospital and school use misguided protocols → preventable crowding and persecution of innocent coughers.

# II. NEW GEOGRAPHY, CLOCKS, AND RESOURCES

### The production map (fictional; all travel times to be fixed with a drawn map)

```
                   RUSSIA / ULAN-UDE
                           |
                     border highway
                           |
               HÜREL'S CAFÉ / wrecked KAMAZ
                           |
            rural turnoff -- old repeater road -- HIGH PASS / COLD STATION
                           |                         ^
                    NARAN TOWN ----------------------|
                  / hospital / school / power station
                           |
                 southern/western evacuation road
```

The map is **not to scale**. The high mountain station is an *invented* staffed weather-and-radio outpost, not a secret military laboratory, a cure, or an enormous government bunker. It has an insulated building, small generator, radio transmitter, weather records, and **limited** food/fuel. One narrow service-road section passes a sheltered thermal drainage / abandoned heating works, the **Warm Cut**: too warm for the infected to freeze. The wider plateau is exposed to severe cold. The station's survival capacity is not infinite.

### Weather and time

Choose **late November**, not a vague “autumn” that becomes −27°C without warning. The weather station reports a credible **approaching blizzard and pass closure**. The timing is a forecast, not supernatural certainty; the road can close earlier or later. It gives the characters a reason to hurry without an arbitrary “gates close on Day 21” device.

### Transport continuity

A **UAZ-452-style gasoline-powered ambulance/utility van** can move the newborn, Oyun, injured Dorj and critical supplies over the main part of the journey; most other travelers ride horses or walk. Everyone cannot fit in a single van. Every vehicle needs keys, fuel, an ignition that works, and a human who can drive it. It can run out of fuel or get stuck. Horses need water, rest and forage; they don't casually gallop for three minutes beside 45-km/h runners without strain. The newborn needs formula/feeding, clean water, warmth and frequent care. It is *not* reasonable to carry a newborn on horseback for days in severe cold with no shelter.

**Fuel distinction:** the gasoline UAZ cannot be refueled from Ganaa's **diesel** KAMAZ. At the café the group scavenges gasoline from a separate abandoned petrol vehicle or documented sealed roadside storage; the KAMAZ's diesel is only useful for appropriate heaters/generators, if those machines are also specified as diesel.

**Key resources to track in every script:** UAZ gasoline; fresh water; infant formula and bottles; lamp/flashlight batteries; the medical dressings; Anu's Hi8 tapes and charged batteries; winter clothing; space in the station. No one discovers a useful item by magic. Whenever a resource increases, show who gave it up.

### Communication, 1998

Use working or overloaded **landlines and switchboards in town**, scheduled **radio relays/shortwave** along isolated roads, handwritten dispatch forms and faxed summaries only where machinery exists. Mobiles existed in Mongolia, but reliable personal rural cell service shouldn't rescue the plot. A camcorder tape cannot instantly be broadcast through the radio: audio must be physically played or recorded onto radio equipment, and photographs require a separate visual path. Batteries go flat; video tape has finite recording duration.

# III. THE FICTIONAL INFECTION RULES — TIGHTENED

These are **fictional laws established by the series**, not claims about real disease.

**Entry:** bite, deep scratch or infected blood into an open wound. Normal coughing, breathing near someone, sharing tea with intact skin, or seeing a red face does not infect anyone. The public falsely associates coughing with infection because of the official messaging.

**Incubation:** body location roughly affects duration. Neck/face potentially ~1 hour; torso several hours; hand/foot often longer, up to about 12 hours. Not a digital countdown. Character timing must be checked from actual *incident* time to *turn* time. Fever, shaking, meat fixation and a tracking red line are cinematic symptoms. Once a bitten person progresses beyond control, there is no spontaneous cure.

**Predation:** an infected attacks to eat. A person who is consumed in the attack remains dead; a bitten person who gets away may turn. Don't casually resurrect a body destroyed by feeding. Dead subjects are not immortal: major brain damage stops them. The distinction is seen gradually, not explained in a speech.

**Exertion:** a recently fed infected can sprint for **roughly** two to three minutes; then it slows and seeks heat. A nearby Standing can rouse from noise, touch or blood, but if unfed it cannot sustain another full-strength chase. Feeding restores energy. Timing differs enough that no one safely relies on exactly 180.00 seconds. The audience can learn patterns; characters can still misjudge.

**Perception:** it reacts to sounds, fresh blood nearby, and visible movement. Sound **does not summon infected from dozens of kilometers away**. A quiet needle through someone's chest is not necessarily instant silence; archery requires aim, distance and recovery time.

**Temperature:** extreme cold (story threshold roughly −20°C sustained long enough) stops movement, **not instantly at a single thermometer reading**. Warmth reanimates frozen bodies when thawing occurs. A cold plateau is safer than a warm culvert, but exposure is just as lethal to human characters. Treat heating pipes as plausible congregating points, not magical homing beacons.

**Wildlife:** do not introduce infected dogs/sheep that turn unless we deliberately establish animal susceptibility. Sheep and carcasses can serve as food and distraction without creating a separate zoological ruleset.

## EP 2 — ХОЁР ХААЛГА / TWO DOORS
**Day 1, 00:30–06:00 · Oyun + Tsetsegmaa · hospital procedural / psychological horror**

**Dramatic question:** Who can the doctor save without abandoning everyone else?

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:45):** A three-year-old has a single new mitten on one hand. Its twin is dangling from her mother's wrist. Around them, birthday confetti sticks to coats dark with blood. Oyun asks someone whether the coughing man was bitten. Two witnesses give contradictory answers. Behind them an orderly starts to cover an apparently dead guest; Oyun catches a faint breath and stops him. It's ordinary triage chaos, not a jump scare.
2. **Setup (0:45–1:45):** Tsetsegmaa has Altan safe in her arms but needs the injured checked. Her first goal is to get out of the hospital with her child. Oyun has an unstable woman in labor, **Narantsetseg**, and multiple dangerous bite patients. She separates injured people from coughing-but-unbitten people. The emergency phone switchboard is jammed.
3. **Pressure (1:45–3:15):** A bitten guest minimizes his wound because he fears confinement. He turns while staff attempt ordinary resuscitation; a doctor is killed. Oyun recognizes the relationship between bite, fever and aggression but doesn't yet know a precise clock. She calls the governor's office and requests an isolation area and transport, not an impossible army instantly at the door.
4. **First set piece (3:15–4:30):** A short, terrible corridor evacuation: rolling hospital beds won't fit around a trolley jam; a nurse must leave the trolley and drag someone. Bolor is bitten through her trouser leg. A recently fed attacker exhausts itself near the hot-water riser. We hold on the changing footfalls rather than someone announcing “three minutes.”
5. **Midpoint (4:30–5:20):** Narantsetseg, already in complicated labor, is bitten while pulling another mother's baby out of danger. **Fetal distress**, confirmed by the midwife, means urgent operative delivery. The infection isn't a miraculous reason to perform a cesarean instantly. Make the operation a sequence unfolding over real elapsed time, compressed by cuts and clock inserts.
6. **Second pressure (5:20–6:45):** The operation has a nurse-anesthetist, light source and limited sterile supplies. During it, Tsetsegmaa discovers the exit she was promised is now blocked by a police quarantine tape. She confronts staff: “Манай охин хазуулаагүй. Яагаад гарахгүй гэж?” This is a grounded conflict, not a shouted manifesto.
7. **Choice / set piece (6:45–8:20):** Naran is born breathing; Narantsetseg asks Oyun to tell Tsogt her name. An infected breach splits maternity into two pockets. Bolor holds a heavy door; Oyun can reach one baby while moving to the maintenance stair. Other mothers are **not mysteriously absent**: we see where they are, with their infants, blocked by the breached corridor. Oyun could wait a little longer to open the passage. She cannot know if those seconds would save or kill everyone. She locks the door on her side to keep the infected from following.
8. **Cliff (8:20–9:00):** Naran whimpers against Oyun's coat. In the hospital car park, figures cluster at the heating plant. Oyun sees a woman through glass holding out an infant; for a split second she almost opens the exit she just sealed. She doesn't. Farther off, Tsetsegmaa, Altan and Dulmaa are escorted into a **separate police-held group** on the town side. Dulmaa escaped the birthday apartment with her daughter and granddaughter and followed them to the hospital. We know they survived this episode; Oyun doesn't.

**Human cost:** Bolor and Narantsetseg are lost; other patients remain behind the door. Oyun's guilt is not whether she was a monster; it's whether she was one minute too early.

**Sound:** muffled birthday music stuck in someone's head; hospital glass; ventilation slowing when backup power engages; enamel bowls rattling on a cart. No giant action score over the key turn.

**New normal:** Officials start treating *all* coughers as potentially infected, despite evidence that bites matter.

## EP 3 — ТАЙВАН БАЙ / STAY CALM
**Day 1–2 · Lkhagva + Sarantuya · bureaucratic thriller / dry black comedy**

**Dramatic question:** What does a frightened official do when telling the truth will end his authority?

1. **Hook:** A municipal radio operator waits for the next sentence of a reassuring announcement. In the governor's office a staff member quietly hands Lkhagva a report of injuries outside the hospital. He covers the typed word “хазуулсан” with his thumb before reading it on air as “өвчлөл.” Same facts, different public reality.
2. **Setup:** An earlier fax / urgent warning from a Russian railway medical contact is in his in-tray, dated **four days before Day 0**. It says: isolate bitten people; suspend unsafe transport; escalate to medical authorities. It is an *unverified but actionable warning*, not definitive proof that Lkhagva knew every detail. Sarantuya logged it and has a retained copy. Lkhagva had routed it for “further verification.”
3. **First pressure:** The provincial fuel reserve will not supply household heating for long if the road is closed, and an immediate evacuation of everyone is physically impossible. The pressure is real. But he uses this truth to justify suppressing information that would let citizens protect themselves. We see a warehouse of fuel he has quietly reserved for his own emergency convoy.
4. **First set piece:** Lkhagva rings the hospital and refuses outside help because police are holding a fuel depot and the ambulance is already dispatched. The phone conversation from Ep 2 is heard only where it adds new information. No full scene replay. Sarantuya watches him put a cross beside the hospital on a district map.
5. **Midpoint:** Citizens assemble in the square after a loudspeaker promises transport. The buses are not coming; one bus is already broken and the other has no driver. Lkhagva says “Үүрээр зохицуулна” because he cannot bring himself to say “байхгүй.” The crowd becomes louder, drawing nearby infected down adjacent alleys. He understands the danger but cannot retract his promise without admitting the lie.
6. **Second pressure:** An arriving group includes Tsetsegmaa and Altan. She gives a policeman a specific account of a person who bit her husband at a roadside café. The report gets stamped as a *public-order incident*, not medical evidence. Sarantuya secretly preserves the original.
7. **Second set piece:** The evacuation isn't a helicopter conveniently waiting on the roof. Lkhagva orders **one UAZ convoy** moved from the municipal garage under an emergency-fuel authorization; his cousin Dashnyam bribes the driver to load personal belongings ahead of radio equipment. A siren starts in the garage while engines are already turning. At the square, the sound attracts a short-range rush. A driver refuses to open the doors when he sees bitten passengers. Lkhagva chooses to leave people behind.
8. **Cliff:** He drives away with Sarantuya, Uyanga, Batu (his bodyguard), and the papers he decided were worth saving. Dashnyam and a driver follow in a second vehicle. Looking back, Sarantuya notices the public sign: “ИРГЭДЭД ҮЙЛЧЛЭХ ТӨВ.” Inside the car Lkhagva tells her to write “тээврийн аюулгүй ажиллагааны журмын дагуу” in his report. She leaves the line blank.

**Human cost:** people who trusted the bus announcement stayed gathered, exposed. Lkhagva is guilty of a **specific documented delay**, not a cartoonishly omniscient conspiracy.

**Humor:** an assistant asks whether the official speech should say “түр хугацаанд” or “тодорхойгүй хугацаагаар.” The governor says, perfectly calmly, “Хүмүүс сонсохыг нь бич.” The joke reveals how he thinks.

**Sound/visual:** rubber stamps, scratchy fax, radio microphone, service doors, two UAZ headlights leaving a crowd in darkness. If the governor escapes, it takes fuel away from someone else.

## EP 4 — НЭГ ГАР / ONE HAND
**Day 1 afternoon recording / Day 2 actual shelter · Anu, Munkh and Tsetsegmaa · found footage**

**Dramatic question:** How much harm can an ordinary teenager cause by going along with a crowd?

1. **Hook:** Anu films her friend Nomin giving a deliberately stupid fake news report about the town's “cold” **earlier on Day 0**, when they still think it's funny. Cut to the same classroom Day 2: now mattresses cover the floor and blankets are taped over windows. The joke has acquired a horrible second meaning. The battery indicator is **recording time available, not an elapsed-world-time clock**.
2. **Setup:** School No. 2 is a **temporary public shelter**, not a school inexplicably teaching algebra during an outbreak. Teachers have a register; parents arrive with children, including Tsetsegmaa and Altan. Some pupils have stove-smoke coughs. The principal has been told to separate “symptomatic” arrivals but received no useful diagnostic instructions.
3. **Pressure:** A boy, Munkh, coughs after helping unload a bag of coal. The principal makes a frightened decision: those who cough must sleep in the separated workshop across the yard. Anu knows he has coughed for years because of smoke, but filming is easier than making an enemy of the group. A girl with a concealed real bite is permitted to stay with the “healthy” crowd.
4. **First set piece:** The principal asks students and adults to approve the separation with a show of hands. She turns an order into apparent democratic consent. Munkh recognizes Anu's raised hand through the little Hi8 screen. This isn't a serious epidemiological vote; it's a leader outsourcing blame to scared children.
5. **Midpoint:** From the annex, Munkh sees a red-faced pupil left inside the main building because she wasn't coughing. He points through glass. No one trusts him. Anu rewinds a few seconds of her tape and sees that she filmed the girl hiding a bleeding calf, but didn't notice in person.
6. **Second pressure:** Tsetsegmaa tries to take Altan out of the school. The nearest exit is padlocked from the outside by police who believe they're keeping the shelter safe. A teacher has a key to the service gate but worries he'll be blamed if anyone leaves. A coughing child begins to cry because everyone has moved away from him. Anu stops recording for the first time, without announcing why.
7. **Second set piece:** The bitten pupil turns among the “healthy” group, making their false division lethal. A teacher tries to unlock a storage room; keys tangle; the door catches on a metal chair. The workshop outside is still locked. Anu has a physical choice: carry the camera or use both hands to help a smaller child through a window. She passes the camera to Munkh through the fence. He drops the latch to let them out despite having been excluded. No triumphant forgiveness speech.
8. **Cliff:** Anu retrieves the camera. The last seconds of a tape show her own raised hand in a trophy-case reflection, followed by her failure to help. In the yard, she hears Munkh coughing beside her, alive. He doesn't speak to her. The recording ends mid-breath.

**Human cost:** students and teachers die because a real bite was hidden inside the “safe” group; Munkh survives, but his trust in Anu is broken. We keep guilt without making an innocent boy's death the only emotional shortcut.

**Production image:** chalked names on desks to mark assigned sleeping spots, smoky breath inside classrooms, the camcorder's greenish low-light picture used only if the chosen **pre-1999 model** supports it.

**Humor:** awkward adolescent teasing before danger; under pressure, a boy asks if the principal is going to mark them absent for surviving. A classmate silently gives him an exhausted look. Move on—no punchline soundtrack.

## EP 5 — ХҮНИЙ ҮНЭ / THE PRICE OF A PERSON
**Day 2 night–Day 3 morning · Erdene + Anu + Munkh · checkpoint siege / moral test**

**Dramatic question:** When does obeying an order become making the decision yourself?

1. **Hook:** Erdene is told to aim at a person in a fog bank. Through his iron sights he sees a man using both hands to hold his jacket closed. A sergeant shouts “Бууд!” We cut before the shot and return to the scene from the earlier hours.
2. **Setup:** An official road cordon sorts traffic using **limited vehicles, supplies and visible bite checks**. Lieutenant Bayar has turned the scarcity into bribes: the gold ring, a sack of flour, extra fuel. He doesn't casually fire a rifle in every civilian's face; his power lies in how everyone depends on his stamp.
3. **Pressure:** A coughing family is refused while a familiar official plate passes untouched. Erdene asks why. Jargal, another conscript, says: “Чиний анхных юм уу? Дараа нь бүр амархан болно.” He is half-joking about corruption and half-describing what the job has done to him.
4. **First set piece:** Anu, Munkh, Tsetsegmaa and Altan arrive among evacuees. Munkh has a smoke cough; Anu has the school footage proving how misleading it is. Erdene makes the practical choice to physically examine for bites instead of relying on a cough. Bayar is angered because an exception makes his pricing system look arbitrary.
5. **Midpoint:** The man at the front of the queue has a progressing red line hidden at his wrist. Erdene notices. The man whispers that if everyone knows, they will lock him in the field. Erdene gives him distance and time rather than telling the lieutenant. The **concealment** is Erdene's understandable and consequential mistake.
6. **Second pressure:** Tsetsegmaa chooses the **westbound civilian agricultural convoy** for herself, Altan and Dulmaa: it is more suited to taking a three-year-old than the cold mountain road. She tells Anu the location of Ganaa's KAMAZ, which she learned from the livestock driver who brought him to town and later gave police his account. She doesn't know about the satchel. Anu and Munkh cannot join because the checkpoint insists that the visibly coughing teenager remain behind. Erdene agrees to get the two teenagers to the next safe relay shelter. Tsetsegmaa's story has a **shown next action**, rather than vanishing.
7. **Second set piece:** The bitten man turns in the narrow queue lane. The crowd's crush and a nearby shot awaken exhausted infected at a roadside culvert. Erdene has to fire at the **same man he chose not to report**, making the hook emotionally coherent. Jargal is separated, not killed solely for a one-liner. Bayar departs in an official jeep with stolen supplies because the chain of command is collapsing, not because he's superhuman.
8. **Cliff:** Erdene, Anu and Munkh reach the ridge on foot with a stolen/abandoned ration bag and rifle. A woman, **Narantuya**, stops them with an arrow. A runner struggles up behind them; the arrow moves away from Erdene toward the infected. A new team is formed in one motion.

**Human cost:** Erdene knows saving someone from public punishment did not save him from turning. This guilt must not be cheaply “cured” by a heroic rescue.

**Humor:** Bayar calmly prices a trip like a market trader; the comedy should reveal an institution, not turn him into a cackling cartoon. Protect the ring scene: “Бөгж чинь гурил болчихдог юм уу?”

## EP 6 — ЭСГИЙ / FELT
**Day 3–4 · Tsogt, Sarnai, Dorj, Narantuya, Erdene · northern road / family horror**

**Dramatic question:** Will Tsogt turn back to save the family already with him, or keep going for the wife he may already have lost?

1. **Hook:** Narantuya puts down the runner behind Erdene. The arrow doesn't magically kill a creature from two hundred meters; it is a short, difficult shot, followed by a struggle. Tsogt sees the soldier's dirty rifle and asks whether he's been bitten. Sarnai, ten, is watching the man's hands, not the uniform.
2. **Setup:** Tsogt's family has come from a herding camp because the last reliable radio message named injured civilians taken to **Naran Hospital**. Narantsetseg is near term and didn't return. Tsogt won't abandon her on a rumor. Old Dorj thinks they should rescue the living in front of them first. They have three horses, a pack animal, and limited supplies; avoid infinite horses or magically silent felt hooves.
3. **Pressure:** Erdene can identify an alternate hospital delivery road; he needs shelter and wants to atone for the checkpoint. Anu has local maps from the school and a camera recording real events. Munkh challenges Erdene's trustworthiness instead of instantly befriending him.
4. **First set piece:** One fed infected intercepts the pack horse near a culvert. They have to lead animals over loose rock, not gallop like in a music video. Sarnai notices it faltering after extended pursuit. She checks an ordinary watch and relates the rhythm to race counting. **First hypothesis, not proof of an exact law.**
5. **Midpoint:** A failed culvert crossing and two exhausted infected block the clean detour. Dorj can attempt the steep footpath but has an injured knee, or the group can turn back and lose hours. He tells them to take the child and medicine. Tsogt refuses to leave him, revealing a conflict about family obligation that cannot be resolved by one brave speech.
6. **Second pressure:** A shout from a trapped traveler attracts nearby infected. Dorj recognizes a chance to draw them toward an empty creek bed beside a **collapsed culvert**. He makes the smallest gesture of a wrestler's **eagle dance** to signal Sarnai to get the horses moving—not a slow-motion fight against one hundred enemies.
7. **Second set piece:** Dorj bangs a metal trough to pull two dangerous infected toward the creek bed, then uses the damaged culvert as a choke point while Erdene and Tsogt drag the old man back to safety. They cannot save the pack horse or most of its stores. Dorj's knee is badly twisted, and he can no longer ride unsupported. **He survives instead of being sacrificed the minute we meet him.** Their success leaves the entire group slower and short of supplies.
8. **Cliff:** At night the town's heating plant glows below. Sarnai helps Dorj adjust the splint under his trouser leg; he pretends it doesn't hurt. A single weak light is still burning by the hospital's boiler annex. Their goal now has a visible address, and it may still be occupied. The family heads toward the hospital.

**Human cost:** the pack horse and supplies are lost, Dorj's knee is damaged, and the journey slows. Tsogt chooses not to abandon a living father while risking the chance to find his wife. Sarnai sees that a survival trick has costs even when everyone comes out alive.

**Humor:** Dorj's dry criticism of young men who argue about maps while refusing to walk the road. The older man's language has weight and little slang.

## EP 7 — НЭР / A NAME
**Day 4–5 · Oyun + Tsogt + Sarnai · rescue / family heartbreak**

**Dramatic question:** What do you tell someone who has ridden into a dead town to find his wife?

1. **Hook:** Oyun hears hooves outside the boiler house where she has sheltered with newborn Naran. She nearly attacks through the door. When Tsogt says his wife's name, she recognizes it from the medical chart; she cannot make herself answer immediately.
2. **Setup:** Oyun has kept Naran alive with formula from a small clinical emergency supply, boiled water and heat from the boiler room. **Show the supply dwindling** and the failed effort to reach radio help. Erdene and the riders have a route in, not a magical rescue chopper.
3. **Pressure:** Tsogt asks to see the ward. Oyun wants to leave before exhausted infected hear the horses. She explains what happened in an ugly, factual way; she doesn't deliver an elegant apology. Sarnai watches Oyun's hands around the infant and senses there is more to the story.
4. **First set piece:** Crossing the ambulance bay. They test sound with a dropped screw; a nearby Standing stirs weakly. They use distance, covered movement and a slow detour, not total immunity from felt-padded hooves. Munkh secures a service gate. Anu records only fragments because the tape and battery are low.
5. **Midpoint:** They glimpse Narantsetseg **inside the sealed maternity wing**, through a wired-glass service window from the exterior passage. She stands motionless near a warm riser. Her hospital bracelet and bitten shoulder identify her. The sealed wing remains sealed; she has not mysteriously escaped into the car park. Tsogt reaches toward the glass and stops. Oyun tells him she delivered a girl. Tsogt quietly asks: “Нэрийг нь юу гэж өгсөн?” The answer is “Наран.”
6. **Second pressure:** Tsogt can try to bring his wife with them as if she were sick—or accept that bringing a responsive infected near a newborn is a death sentence. Sarnai holds the baby for the first time. He chooses not to touch his wife again. A small, adult refusal carries more pain than a speech.
7. **Second set piece:** With Dorj unable to ride, the group must free the hospital's battered **UAZ ambulance/utility van**. It has fuel for a limited distance, a cold engine and one spare infant-formula tin in the dispensary. The UAZ is assigned to the hospital utility fleet and has enough room for the infant, doctor, the injured Dorj and equipment, while the able-bodied riders stay with the horses. Oyun returns briefly inside, risking time to obtain the formula and a patient chart. A nearby mechanical generator backfires, awakening a few Standings. Erdene creates a quiet escape route; the van starts after an ugly, suspenseful delay.
8. **Cliff:** A scheduled radio message comes through the UAZ: the old **High Pass weather station** still answers, and the forecast says a blizzard may close the upper access road in days. Through the wired-glass window behind them, Tsogt catches one last glimpse of his wife's unmoving silhouette. He doesn't turn back. Sarnai holds the baby tighter.

**Human cost:** Tsogt meets his second daughter and loses the possibility of seeing her mother alive. Oyun must live among people who know what she left behind.

**Sound:** boiler pops, felt against metal, idling engine that refuses to catch, one infant's ordinary breathing. Avoid scoring the wife as a monster reveal.

## EP 8 — ХҮРЭЛИЙН ГУАНЗ / THE CAFÉ
**Day 5–6 · Anu + Oyun + Erdene · evidence horror / roadside siege**

**Dramatic question:** Is proof of the truth useful after so many people have already paid for the lie?

1. **Hook:** An old roadside sign bangs on one chain. We recognize Hürel's café before characters do. A blackened KAMAZ stands beside it, windshield fractured. Someone has written **“БУУЗ”** on a scrap of cardboard inside, a remnant of an ordinary business.
2. **Setup:** The group chooses the café intentionally: it lies on the same road as the high-pass turnoff, Tsetsegmaa told Anu where Ganaa's truck was lost, and they need **gasoline from an abandoned petrol vehicle**, infant water, tools and a safe indoor stopping point. They **don't** randomly stumble onto the one convenient clue in Mongolia.
3. **Pressure:** They search only accessible sections. The burnt café is unstable; they cannot loot a raging fire two days after the fact. Anu finds Hürel's worn ledger, where Ganaa's unpaid buuz are still written. Munkh makes a small remark about debts that stops being funny when they find the table where Hürel died.
4. **First set piece:** Three drained infected stand in the machinery shed near residual engine warmth. Erdene blocks the narrow door; the group must move a tank drum without scraping it. They discover the strongest Standing cannot sprint far without fresh meat. Nobody declares it a confirmed biological law.
5. **Midpoint:** Inside Ganaa's scorched KAMAZ, Anu finds Irina's wired-shut satchel, tapes and a written Naran medical-office address. Oyun recognizes the instructions as exactly what her hospital needed. A **copy of an earlier warning is missing**: the details in the satchel imply a report had already been sent. This is a question, not a speech.
6. **Second pressure:** Irina packed a **battery-powered cassette recorder** with her tapes; its aging cells work intermittently, leaving time for only part of the recordings. Anu's camcorder uses **different batteries**, and nobody swaps them as a shortcut. The tape describes observed burst duration, bite transmission and the danger of warm buildings. It also mentions an injured young woman leaving a freight holding area. Dasha was not a random disease oracle; she was another frightened person who went somewhere she should not have.
7. **Second set piece:** As they refuel, a practical human mistake—a dropped metal funnel against concrete—wakes infected close by. They cannot count on a cinematic explosion. To escape, they leave half the **salvaged gasoline** and take the UAZ through the outer gate while Narantuya buys space from a rooftop with limited arrows. Sarnai times the fleeing creature's slowdown with the watch; the second observation supports her earlier hypothesis.
8. **Cliff:** In the KAMAZ cab, behind the visor, a picture of Ganaa and Altan. Anu recognizes Altan from the school and finally understands how these separate lives connect. Oyun reads Irina's warning aloud only to the other adults: *the message was intended for the hospital before the birthday.* The episode ends on Oyun's face, not a new monster.

**Human cost:** the group saves the evidence but loses usable fuel and an easy route. Oyun understands that what happened in the ward was not inevitable in the simple way she hoped.

**Humor:** one unforced echo of the Ganaa–Hürel debt joke in the ledger. It creates grief through familiarity.

## EP 9 — ХЭН МЭДСЭН БЭ / WHO KNEW?
**Day 6–8 · Oyun + Lkhagva + Sarantuya · confrontation without a shootout**

**Dramatic question:** What if the man who failed everyone still controls what they need to survive?

1. **Hook:** A vehicle is stuck across the only service-road bridge ahead. A familiar voice politely asks the group to help push. Lkhagva stands beside the UAZ he commandeered from town; his bodyguard has a concealed bite. He is tired, dirty and very much alive.
2. **Setup:** The governor's convoy spent Days 2–5 at a southern road-control post waiting for promised military transport that never arrived. When the post ran out of heat and rations, they doubled back to the weather-station service road using the same scheduled radio message as the others. They now reach the lower service road nearly out of gasoline after that long detour, not from a convenient helicopter crash. Sarantuya has his dispatch folder and an unsubmitted copy of the four-day-old warning. Everyone knows he could authorize a small reserve of station diesel if the weather relay accepts his code; everyone has a reason to hate him.
3. **Pressure:** Oyun shows him Irina's tapes and demands why the warning was classified as a rumor. He answers carefully: the report was unverified, the fuel supply was fragile, and the central office told him not to panic the town. Every argument contains part of the truth, which makes his failure more disturbing.
4. **First set piece:** They need to cross a culvert beside a warm industrial drainage pipe. On the other side, recently fed infected are awake. Bayar—now serving as a self-appointed road marshal farther up—is rationing access and demands fuel. He is an obstacle with a physical interest, not a supervillain teleporting between locations.
5. **Midpoint:** Sarantuya produces the stamped original: Lkhagva was asked to **isolate bite victims and stop crowded evacuations** four days before the first Naran birthday outbreak. His delay is demonstrable. He never had proof of a worldwide epidemic. This makes his responsibility specific, and keeps the larger origins mysterious.
6. **Second pressure:** The bodyguard admits his wound after hiding it. He asks not to be shot while still fully himself. Oyun requests a bounded isolation watch, weapons outside, and a time record. Lkhagva initially wants him disposed of. Tsogt says very little; his refusal to treat the man as luggage complicates everyone's feelings.
7. **Second set piece:** The bodyguard progresses faster than expected. A rushed attempt to move him across the culvert fails. Anu's video is blocked by two adults arguing over his restraint. A noise pulls several nearby Standings awake and the group must drop the governor's heavy personal cases to hold the crossing. **They survive by cooperating with someone they don't forgive.**
8. **Cliff:** The high weather relay transmits a simple warning: if the upper road isn't crossed before the leading storm arrives, the station cannot send a vehicle down again. The Warm Cut lies directly beneath the last usable bridge. Sarantuya finally asks Lkhagva whether she should burn the stamped fax. He says nothing. She puts it inside Anu's camera bag.

**Human cost:** the governor's authority becomes morally empty but materially useful; the group gives up the satisfaction of immediately punishing him. Sarantuya stops being an accessory to his version of events.

**Humor:** Lkhagva asks whether the videotape “can be filed properly.” Anu replies, “Танд өгвөл л алга болно биз.” One dry exchange, no applause.

## EP 10 — ХҮЙТНИЙ ЦААНА / BEYOND THE COLD
**Day 8–10 · Sarnai, Tsogt, Oyun and Anu · thermal choke point / moral finale**

**Dramatic question:** Will the survivors build a safer place, or reproduce the same choices that destroyed Naran?

1. **Hook:** Sunrise. At the high ridge the thermometer is deep below zero; one infected figure is locked stiff in the frost. A few hundred meters lower in the steaming cut, another is moving normally. The difference is terrifying precisely because the rules still make sense.
2. **Setup:** The insulated weather station is visible across the final traverse. The storm front is a few hours away, not a gate set by an arbitrary calendar. The UAZ can no longer pass a damaged section; the travelers must take the newborn and formula by covered sled or on foot for a **short, measured final distance**, with the injured Dorj helped by two people. The weather station has three staff and one heated main room designed for about a dozen people; the travelers, governor's convoy and Bayar's followers exceed that capacity. A separate vestibule can be warmed at a real fuel cost.
3. **First pressure:** Munkh coughs after inhaling smoke near the thermal works. Bayar, who has reached the passage ahead, uses it to deny him access and demands a health certificate he knows no one can produce. Anu's earlier silence returns as an actual decision. She says what she saw at the school, and Oyun checks for a bite. He is kept in monitored separation from confirmed injured people, not condemned for an ordinary cough.
4. **First set piece:** To move people up the narrow slope they need to shut a manually controlled warm-air exhaust that attracts nearby Standing. The mechanism is on the **warm** side of a maintenance cage. Erdene and Narantuya create a limited diversion with dropped scrap and covered lamps, not a limitless ammunition sequence.
5. **Midpoint:** The governor tells the station crew to secure the inner door and stop intake. It is almost the exact decision he forced onto Oyun at the hospital. Oyun now has a workable alternative: a screened outer vestibule, timed entry in small groups, and a manned escape route. She must use precious fuel, light and staff time. She chooses the harder procedure and takes responsibility aloud, in ordinary clinical language.
6. **Second pressure:** Steam blows back through the warm cut. A half-fed infected reaches the old manual control as the insulated gate sticks. The person who can reach the lever cannot rejoin the group before the mechanical catch drops. Tsogt is in the best position, having worked such heavy machinery around farms. No one is chosen by destiny; he makes a dreadful practical calculation.
7. **Final set piece:** Tsogt sends Sarnai and Dorj toward the shelter with her baby sister. He begins a familiar **short horse-training song** softly, partly to keep her moving at a steady rhythm, partly to draw the nearest infected away from the gate; he is too winded to finish it cleanly. Sarnai times the runner's weakening with her watch and shouts a warning to Erdene, who catches the latch from the safe side. As the others reach the upper stairs Tsogt forces the manual lever into place from inside the warm cage. Dorj, held back by his knee, understands too late what his son has chosen; Sarnai has to physically stop her grandfather from going back down. The last bars of his song break off behind the door. **Cold will stop the infected later; it doesn't save Tsogt now.**
8. **Resolution and last image:** The remaining group gets into the station. They are not cured or triumphant: Naran needs feeding; Oyun needs to clean wounds; Sarantuya preserves the fax; Anu holds a camera whose picture is now worth more than the governor's report. Lkhagva is permitted to coordinate fuel because he knows the registers and refuses to surrender the role—survival is not poetic justice. Sarnai starts to count the people indoors, stops, and includes Munkh behind the observation glass. Outside, new snow covers the warm cut. A faint wet drip begins beneath an iced-over pipe. Winter holds them safe **for now**; the thaw will undo that protection.

**Human cost:** Tsogt dies securing the mechanical route; Dorj lives to see it and must help raise the children. Survival now carries the burden of what the others choose to remember and record.

**Final emotion:** not “we won”; **“we finally told each other the truth, and it still cost something.”**

# VI. CHARACTER SEASON ARCS — SIMPLE, PLAYABLE, NOT THESIS SPEECHES

- **Ganaa (EP1; echo EP8):** generous, funny, proud, avoidant. His mistake is hiding a bite because he wants one birthday. He is neither the unique source of the epidemic nor an evil patient zero. The lingering image is a father's gift on a table.
- **Tsetsegmaa and Altan (EP1–5):** Tsetsegmaa is not disposable grief. She identifies what happened at home, resists unjust detention, protects Altan, informs Anu of the cafe, and chooses a different evacuation route on-screen. Their fate beyond that convoy is open, **not presented as secretly resolved**.
- **Oyun (EP2, 7–10):** she once closed a door while begging herself there was no choice. At the station she builds an alternative, even though it is expensive and still imperfect. She can use humor with Bolor early, then stop entirely when the nurse is doomed.
- **Sarnai (EP6–10):** notices patterns because she has learned timing through horse races. Her arc is not becoming an emotionless prodigy; she learns that counting keeps other people alive while not making loss fair.
- **Tsogt (EP6–10):** keeps moving toward his wife when his living family needs him. He is forced to accept the death he cannot undo, then chooses a deliberate sacrifice for the children he can protect. He talks little, with ordinary words.
- **Erdene (EP5–10):** follows orders, then hides a real risk out of kindness, then shoots the person. His growth is neither “always follow rules” nor “ignore every rule”; it is making a decision with evidence and consequences.
- **Anu (EP4–10):** treats the camera as protection from responsibility. Her raised hand isolates a friend; by the finale she actively defends him and preserves documentary evidence. Filming alone never saves anyone.
- **Munkh (EP4–10):** falsely branded infectious; won't grant instant forgiveness to Anu. He survives to become a person instead of a lesson. Dry, cautious, intelligent, capable of anger.
- **Lkhagva (EP3, 9–10):** intelligent, afraid, evasive. His logistical concerns are often partly true; he uses them to obscure specific negligence. He survives and retains usefulness, which is more frightening than a theatrical villain death.
- **Sarantuya (EP3, 9–10):** begins as the person who knows which forms went where; ends by choosing which record cannot disappear. Not a heroic whistleblower overnight.
- **Bayar (EP5, 9–10):** exploits fear and scarcity in procedural language. He has to travel and barter like any human; no teleporting villain.
- **Dorj (EP6–10):** elder with a few distinctive gestures and a lived-in bond with Sarnai. **He survives** the culvert diversion with an injured knee; we resist killing an elder minutes after introducing him. His survival imposes a transport cost, and in the finale he has to accept the death of his own son and become the children’s remaining family.

# VII. ATMOSPHERE, COMEDY AND MONGOLIAN DIALOGUE

### Sensory rules

**Town / before collapse:** ordinary voices overlap in small rooms; radiator ticks; wet boots; oil-stained hands; children nagging parents; radio playing too loud; kettle boiling; a neighbor returns an empty bottle. Horrific content becomes powerful when it invades somewhere specific and loved.

**During collapse:** the lights remain on in some buildings while others fail; a kettle keeps whistling in an abandoned kitchen; a stamp sits beside an unattended radio; loudspeakers repeat useless instructions; the heating plant turns into a visual gathering point for infected and survivors. Don't show generic empty streets every episode.

**Road:** engine rattle, wind against canvas, horses shifting weight, radiator hoses, condensation forming in the van, a lullaby heard through cabin metal. The landscape isn't just pretty. It costs time, warmth and food to cross.

**Mountain finale:** sudden silence in deep cold; exposed skin freezing, short breath, stiffened fingers fumbling a gate. Warm industrial drainage is **visibly dangerous**, a sharply different ecosystem at nearly the same elevation.

### A few new tonal examples (ILLUSTRATIVE, not locked screenplay dialogue)

**EP3 — dry official black comedy:**

**ТУСЛАХ:** Дарга аа, энэ мэдэгдэл дээр “түр хугацаанд” гэж бичих үү?

**ЛХАГВА** *(without looking up)*: Ялгаа байна уу?

**ТУСЛАХ:** Хүмүүс асуугаад байна.

**ЛХАГВА:** Тэгвэл “түр” гэчих. Дараа нь тодорхой болгоно биз.

He has not cracked a joke; his evasiveness is the joke.

**EP5 — friends under stress:**

**ЖАРГАЛ** *(low; glancing at the dirty checkpoint kettle)*: Эндхийн цай чинь хүртэл хилийн шалгалтаар ордог байх аа.

**ЭРДЭНЭ:** Дуугүйхэн уучих.

**ЖАРГАЛ:** Халуун бол ууна. Энэ чинь өчигдрийнх шиг байна.

Don't add another gag. A sudden, ordinary interruption should end it.

**EP7 — Tsogt hears his daughter's name:**

**ЦОГТ:** ...Нэрийг нь өгсөн үү?

**ОЮУН:** Наран гэж.

**ЦОГТ:** ...Өөрөө хэлсэн үү?

**ОЮУН:** Тийм.

He looks at the child. No speech about destiny, light, or darkness.

**EP10 — Erdene's curse is earned:** the quiet “...Пизда.” remains a signature of what the soldier is too exhausted to explain. Do not replace profanity with elegant substitutes just to sound cinematic.

### Performance constraints

Write the actor's **intention**, not labels on every line: “tries to prevent the child from hearing,” “wants to end the call before the secretary sees the paper,” “speaks like this is a normal clinical task even though it isn't.” Silence is part of performance. Reserve shouting for someone who must physically overcome noise or genuinely loses control.

# VIII. 1998 RESEARCH ANCHORS / WHAT IS DOCUMENTED VS INVENTED

**Documented, usable atmosphere:** Mongolia's mobile telecommunications licensing started in the 1990s, with mobile and Internet service introduced in 1996; radio relay infrastructure and limited fixed infrastructure were significant in the late 1990s (Ministry of Digital Development history: https://mddic.gov.mn/mn/монгол-улсын-харилцаа-холбоо-мэдээл/ ; Communications Regulatory Commission: https://admin.crc.gov.mn/timeline/history-of-the-organization/en). This supports a landline/radio/physical-paper plot; it does **not** prove that all telephones in our fictional town were out.

**Documented, usable hospital backdrop:** 1998 Mongolia had a transitioning public-health system, and hospital reform had begun. Public hospital institutions and constrained service delivery are historically grounded; the exact hospital scene, procedure, staffing, and isolated ward remain fictional and need medical consultation (IMF 1999 country review: https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/1999/004/article-A001-en.xml; World Bank study: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/736861468191042111/pdf/103721-WP-P148340-Understanding-and-Monitoring-Service-Delivery-in-a-Decentralizing-Environment-in-Mongolia-PUBLIC.pdf).

**Documented, usable climate:** Mongolian autumn temperatures vary sharply; late November and mountain cold are more plausible than imposing peak-winter conditions in early October. Specific storm arrival in this fictional story is **invented** (UNFCCC Mongolia communication: https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/MONGOLIA%20FOURTH%20NC%202024.pdf).

**Documented camera constraint:** Sony's historical timeline identifies a 1998 NightShot-equipped DCR-TRV9 model, while Digital8 launches cited in Sony's January 1999 release occurred in **1999**, so don't put a 1999 Digital8 camera into 1998. Select and visually model **one concrete period-appropriate Hi8/Video8-era model** after prop verification; generic “green night vision” should not force an impossible camera. Sony sources: https://www.sony.jp/handycam/INFO/history/index.html ; https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/News/Press/199901/99-001B/.

**Invented on purpose:** Naran town, the station, warm cut, radio broadcasts, precise route and meteorology, individual characters, disease mechanics, and government decisions. They are **story design**, not historical claims.

# IX. WHAT WE SHOULD WRITE NEXT, IN ORDER

1. **Freeze EP1** after timing and bite continuity are checked. Keep its dialogue as the voice reference, rather than “improving” it into clean schoolbook Mongolian.
2. Draw a **one-page location and road map** plus a 10-day timetable. This is a hard prerequisite for believable travel and chance meetings.
3. Make **Episode 2 a fully revised screenplay** with emotional blocking and realistic elapsed time. Its first two minutes should prove that Episode 1 mattered.
4. Conduct a **character whereabouts / wounds / vehicle-fuel / baby-food / camera-tape** continuity audit after each script.
5. After Episodes 2–3, test 8–10 minute animatic timing. If the hospital episode cannot fit, cut a subplot; don't speed-run surgery or increase dialogue playback unnaturally.
6. Only then turn the remaining eight treatments into acting-directed scripts. The finale may evolve, but each setup must get a consequence.

**Creative status:** a coherent and research-informed **proposed** new season, not final locked canon. Larger departures from the prior bible (governor's helicopter removed, Munkh and Dorj survive, faster Day 0–10 arc, late-November storm replaces Day 21 gates) are transparent editorial choices for the user to approve or revise.
