# ХАНИАД — THE COUGH
## EPISODE 1: «АЯГАА ХУВААЛЦ» / SHARE THE BOWL
### Pilot story treatment v4.0 — 25:00 target

**Status:** New dramatic treatment, **not** locked screenplay or approved dialogue. This deliberately proposes replacing v3.0's ~9-minute Episode 1 duration with a 25-minute pilot, while preserving its principal events and future continuity. The timed performance read and animatic will determine the real finished runtime.

**Genre:** lived-in Mongolian dark comedy → quiet psychological dread → sudden physical horror. **Setting:** fictional Naran, northern Mongolia, late November 1998; Russian freight yard and border road. English scene directions; any examples of speech are in Mongolian Cyrillic and are *not* substitutes for the approved v2.1 lines.

**Core logline:** A generous, evasive truck driver trying to keep a promise to his daughter unknowingly carries an urgent warning—and a fatal bite—back to a town that still thinks tomorrow will be ordinary. In the final seconds he may be protecting his child, or losing the last of himself.

**Driving emotional contradiction:** Ganaa is someone who always says, “Дараа болъё,” even about serious problems. He is not a saint. His jokes and generosity make him loved, but his avoidance destroys the tiny margin of time in which he might have protected his family. He is responsible for choices, but not for inventing the disease or for the government ignoring earlier warnings.

**Audience promise:** By the end of the pilot we should care what happens to Tsetsegmaa and Altan, remember Hürel as a person rather than an attack victim, sense that Naran existed before the camera arrived, and have two urgent questions: **Is Ganaa still in control?** and **Why was the town already being told it was “flu”?** The fate of the birthday household is answered promptly in Episode 2; the earlier-warning mystery develops across the season.

## Decisions and continuity that must stay intact

- Irina's documents are meant for a duty doctor / health office, not magically delivered to Ulaanbaatar that day. Her cassette and papers stay behind the driver's seat in the **disabled, not incinerated** KAMAZ at Hürel's café and become findable again in Episode 8.
- Dasha says she is visiting her sister in Naran. Ganaa and Dasha speak basic Russian with misunderstandings; she is never inexplicably fluent in his Mongolian remarks.
- The shared **enamel bowl is not how infection passes**. The infection is from Dasha's later deep wrist bite. No fabricated contagious cough scene.
- The infected are drawn to movement, sound, fresh blood and food, then heat when exhausted. The viewer *sees* an abnormal sprint/stop, but does not receive a scientific lecture.
- The sheep sequence is silent: Ganaa fixates on a sheep's throat, is caught by his own reflection, and says **no** contrived self-explanation.
- Preserve the line **“Томуу гэнэ ээ? Хүрэлийг тэр чинь хазаад... идчихсэн шүү дээ. Ямар алных нь томуу вэ...”** word for word.
- Altan is **three years old**. The crying infant at the party belongs to a visiting guest and sleeps in a separate bedroom. Establish this on screen, not in a retrospective explanation.
- Tsetsegmaa, Altan and Dulmaa survive the initial assault and continue through Episodes 2–5. Ganaa **does not** turn into an articulate half-zombie, receive a cure, or secretly survive the season by convenience.
- At the birthday, the party population is credible: roughly 40 **visitors over the evening**, not 40 adults crammed simultaneously into a tiny apartment. Use the adjacent stair landing and kitchen, with approximately 12–18 people in the late scene.
- Evening clock is a **provisional design assumption**: late-afternoon deep wrist bite, fever over several hours, major loss of control near 21:00. It must pass a final clock audit. The original rule gives variable delay for hand wounds up to ~12 hours, not a guaranteed exact timer.
- Existing registered world geography is retained: Naran ~29 km from café by road (proposed), main route via Upper Junction; town's apartment, market and hospital positions are preserved until graybox tests approve changes.

# FULL 25:00 BEAT SHEET

**Runtime markers below describe screen time, NOT real-world clock time.** Time passes off-screen between scenes; the freight/border drive still takes hours. Each numbered beat should have an objective, a human action, a change in information or emotion, and a useful handoff.

### 01 — 00:00–01:25 (1:25) — A man standing where he shouldn't
**Place:** Ulan-Ude freight yard, predawn. **POV:** security guard, then Irina.
- A guard complains about a smell inside a car and tries to roust what he believes is a drunk trespasser. His irritability is mundane, not comedic villainy. The figure looks wrong: flushed skin, a damp collar, breath like an exhausted sprinter.
- A sudden, shockingly rapid crossing of the yard. We do **not** frame a spectacular zombie kill; use the flashlight spinning into gravel and a metal key still swinging against an open wagon door.
- **Turn:** it appears to be an uncontrollable attacker; seconds later the attacker inexplicably slows and moves toward a steam pipe.
- **Sound:** rail couplings, an idling diesel, one sharp key impact, then steam hiss. Do not score the first 30 seconds like a trailer.
- **Plant:** anomalous sprint, abrupt stop, attraction to warmth. Never explain its cause here.

### 02 — 01:25–02:10 (0:45) — The warning that has nowhere to go
**Place:** dispatch office. **POV:** Irina.
- Irina rewinds a cassette and compares her handwritten timestamps to what she has just seen. The office phone is unusable/answered only by an indifferent intermediary; show a concrete obstacle rather than implying all telephones are dead.
- She writes “Наран / жижүүр эмч” on the envelope, gathers copied documents and two labeled cassettes.
- **Turn:** she cannot rely on the official route; she must entrust the papers to a stranger.
- **Image transition:** the spinning tape spool cuts to a turning market bicycle wheel in Naran. **Title card** over ordinary sound, not over a dramatic explosion.

### 03 — 02:10–03:20 (1:10) — Naran wakes
**Place:** apartment microdistrict → market street. **POV:** town ensemble, anchored to Tsetsegmaa's route.
- Heating plant smoke; an old bus fighting its starter; someone shaking a rug from a balcony; a woman hauling a water can; a ТҮЦ shutters open; kids dodge a puddle; road patchwork under bicycle tires.
- No montage of anonymous nostalgia for its own sake: each object tells us how daily life works. The shop and bus stop will appear again, altered, in later episodes.
- **Turn:** the town feels full of routine, not like an abandoned game level. A radio mentions routine respiratory illness in passing, easy to dismiss.
- **Sound:** footsteps on grit, wood being split, radio under an open window, distant market bargaining.

### 04 — 03:20–04:30 (1:10) — A small debt, a townful of witnesses
**Place:** neighborhood shop / kiosk. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa, shopkeeper and two regulars.
- Tsetsegmaa buys last-minute candles, flour, and an inexpensive birthday sweet. Shopkeeper **Nyamka** keeps a penciled household debt notebook. Tsetsegmaa pays part, asks to write down the remainder; establish money pressure without a speech.
- Regulars **Bataa** (formerly worked with the heating plant) and **Togoo** (odd jobs, sometimes sleeping at friends' places) bicker about promised day work, smoking and who owes whom. Their drinking is observed, not ridiculed; neither is presented as a disposable caricature.
- **Optional texture of natural banter (not locked):** “Өнөөдөр ачлагадаа явахгүй юм уу?” / “Явна аа.” / “Өдөр болчихлоо.” / “Тэгээд яахав, би очиход байж л таарна.”
- **Turn:** a known neighborhood geography and two familiar background faces are established. Later, their reactions to danger can have meaning.
- **Plant:** Nyamka's debt notebook; Bataa's boiler work; old shop phone; reliable location for gathering/rumors.

### 05 — 04:30–05:50 (1:20) — A girl waiting for the door
**Place:** Ganaa's apartment and stair landing. **POV:** Altan and Tsetsegmaa.
- The birthday decorations are handmade and slightly uneven; relatives bring mismatched bowls. Dulmaa is practical, cooking and managing coats. A visiting cousin settles **her infant in the bedroom**, briefly telling everyone not to wake him.
- Altan repeatedly checks the hallway when footsteps come up the stairs. She knows one song her father sings off-key; she hums it while arranging paper candles. She has been promised something small from Russia, not a luxury toy.
- Tsetsegmaa defends Ganaa once, then stops doing it; she knows the pattern of late promises. Her irritation and affection coexist.
- **Turn:** before seeing him, viewers know what Ganaa stands to lose. **Plant:** guest infant, narrow corridor, kitchen doorway, gift/song, crowded stair exit.

### 06 — 05:50–06:45 (0:55) — A man who can talk his way out of anything
**Place:** Ulan-Ude yard, morning. **POV:** Ganaa.
- Ganaa jokes with a loader about a loose cargo strap and borrows a hand rather than asking sincerely. The coworker roasts him back. He checks a child's paper-wrapped present—small mittens—and a photo behind the sun visor.
- He looks at his watch and realizes he's already running late. We see why people enjoy him and how easily he deflects practical responsibility.
- **Turn:** the amusing nuisance is also a father trying to keep a promise.

### 07 — 06:45–07:45 (1:00) — Irina gives him the wrong kind of favor
**Place:** freight yard beside KAMAZ. **POV:** Ganaa.
- In basic Russian, Irina asks him to carry a sealed warning to the duty doctor in Naran, not to the police. He half-understands and repeats “эмнэлэг.” When she insists on urgency, he looks again at his watch, caught between his good nature and the trip ahead.
- She hands him the satchel. He tucks it **behind the driver's seat**, where we see its specific worn clasp. She asks him not to forget; he gives the easy reassuring gesture of a man who promises too quickly.
- **Turn:** Ganaa takes on a second obligation he has no time or plan to fulfill. **Plant/payoff:** intact satchel later recoverable in EP08.

### 08 — 07:45–08:35 (0:50) — The long drive actually takes time
**Place:** border highway / customs waiting area. **POV:** Ganaa.
- No fake teleport: sunlight changes, he stops for paperwork, waits while a tired official checks cargo, fumbles for a stamp, and bargains briefly over a small delay. Use a clock or change of light sparingly; don't draw a false map on screen.
- He uses the waiting time to flatten the mittens' wrapping but not to read Irina's documents. This is avoidance, not ignorance.
- **Turn:** hours have passed, and keeping both promises is becoming harder.

### 09 — 08:35–09:25 (0:50) — The town thinks the problem is normal
**Place:** Naran clinic frontage / market. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa.
- A neighbor coughs from stove smoke. Another neighbor jokes about flu while asking after the birthday. A young worker complains the hospital keeps asking people to phone first, though the phone rarely connects.
- Bataa and Togoo briefly help a customer push an uncooperative vehicle. Their competence undermines the simplistic “drunks = useless” joke.
- **Turn:** spectators hear the language of ordinary illness without seeing the truth yet. **Plant:** later suspicion of coughers will be a community mistake, not a logical diagnosis.

### 10 — 09:25–10:15 (0:50) — Birthday preparations keep going without him
**Place:** apartment kitchen/landing. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa.
- A brief disagreement over whether to cut the cake before Ganaa arrives. Dulmaa offers practical help; Altan insists on waiting because her father promised to sing.
- Tsetsegmaa grudgingly saves his place and moves a saucepan off the stove. The room is warm and cramped; the doorway she keeps crossing will later become the barrier he tries to close.
- **Turn:** waiting becomes an active, slightly painful family choice.

### 11 — 10:15–11:35 (1:20) — The stranger and the bowl
**Place:** road after customs; KAMAZ cabin. **POV:** Ganaa.
- Ganaa sees Dasha walking with a damaged arm and slows reluctantly. She says “сестра, Наран.” He asks the destination twice to be sure; she gives a short answer and avoids other questions.
- He offers his **single enamel tea bowl**. She coughs, drinks, passes it back. A faint stain on the rim unsettles him but he uses the clean side. Sharing the bowl is a gesture of instinctive help, **not transmission**.
- **Turn:** his generosity has put an unknown person within arm's reach. **Plant:** enamel bowl for the title and EP08 echo.

### 12 — 11:35–12:40 (1:05) — Hunger becomes unrecognizable
**Place:** moving cab / brief roadside pause. **POV:** Ganaa, observing Dasha.
- He offers meat intended for Altan's party. She eats like someone who hasn't eaten for days, then with an appetite that no longer resembles ordinary hunger. He jokes in Mongolian by reflex; she can't understand. He gestures for her to leave some; she freezes and looks at him a moment too long.
- He asks about the bandage in broken Russian. She says “собака.” We don't prove whether she is lying.
- **Turn:** he is no longer amused, but still refuses to put her out into the cold. No explanatory zombie speech.

### 13 — 12:40–13:30 (0:50) — An unsettling silence, not another attack
**Place:** moving cab, rural highway. **POV:** Ganaa.
- Dasha's breathing gets fast; her cheeks redden. Then, briefly, she goes oddly quiet against the warm vent. Ganaa notices, turns down the music and watches her hands. She notices his attention and turns away.
- Ganaa considers stopping at a clinic but checks his watch, then says only a simple practical offer (water / sit down), not “you are becoming a monster.”
- **Turn:** Ganaa chooses not to confront what he cannot explain. **Plant:** infected heat-seeking trait, later recognizable.

### 14 — 13:30–14:25 (0:55) — A normal errand seen from home
**Place:** Ganaa's building / shop courtyard. **POV:** Altan and regulars.
- Altan runs outside thinking a rumbling truck is her father's; it is a coal delivery. A child makes fun of her waiting; Tsetsegmaa takes her inside, not scolding the child.
- Nyamka pulls the shutters partway down for evening. Bataa has one sincere, useful exchange with the delivery driver, showing he belongs to this town.
- **Turn:** the town's routine keeps moving while Ganaa's danger approaches unseen. **Visual rhyme:** a truck stopping outside one building, another truck pulling into Hürel's café.

### 15 — 14:25–15:25 (1:00) — An old debt between real friends
**Place:** Hürel's roadside café. **POV:** Ganaa and Hürel.
- Hürel greets Ganaa with the existing unpaid-buuz joke. The two aren't comedians reading punchlines; they move comfortably around the kitchen threshold, knowing what each other will say. A driver waiting for food watches their exchange. Hürel quietly adds extra food for the road despite talking about his debt.
- Show the café's routine: steam on windows, an old ledger in a protected drawer, kettle repair, a mechanic's oil-stained gloves, empty tables waiting for the evening rush. The place has a rear exit and a service yard, not a magical set with a single door.
- **Turn:** Hürel is worth missing. **Plant:** ledger, burned sign, spatial logic for EP08.

### 16 — 15:25–16:25 (1:00) — The comedy doesn't know it's ending
**Place:** café main room. **POV:** the three people.
- Hürel notices Dasha and makes a cheeky comment about Tsetsegmaa. Ganaa tells him “Битгий шаа” in their established joking register. A small laugh; their mundane conversation continues around the kettle and food.
- Dasha isn't performing “zombie symptoms” at the camera. She is drawn to steam, then the smell of food, then the hand that brings it. Hürel reaches to steady her because she looks faint.
- **Turn:** ordinary help is about to become dangerous. Hold the tiny moment when Ganaa recognizes something is wrong before he can do anything.

### 17 — 16:25–17:35 (1:10) — The attack and the bite
**Place:** café main room → doorway → KAMAZ. **POV:** Ganaa.
- Dasha attacks Hürel. The brutality is abrupt, messy, and brief rather than a prolonged gore set piece. Ganaa first freezes, then moves toward his friend, grabs a chair and discovers it doesn't stop her.
- He runs to the truck, cannot start it immediately, and Dasha catches the driver's door and bites deeply into his wrist. The time and exact wound are explicitly logged on a continuity card.
- **Turn:** Ganaa loses his friend and the safety of his own body. **Sound:** chair scraping, truck starter, only then a sudden, horrible scream.

### 18 — 17:35–18:30 (0:55) — The wrong escape
**Place:** café forecourt / ditch. **POV:** Ganaa.
- The damaged KAMAZ sideswipes an old fuel dispenser and stops against a concrete protector. Spilled petrol is ignited by a **visibly plausible** ignition source to be settled in safety/FX blocking; do not rely on a magical friction spark. The fire damages the café frontage and disables the truck but doesn't swallow the entire cab.
- Ganaa gets out the passenger side and tries once to reach Hürel. The heat forces him back. A passing livestock driver pulls up and offers a ride. Ganaa hides the injury.
- **Turn:** he escapes, but Irina's satchel and the only evidence of what happened stay with the truck.

### 19 — 18:30–19:15 (0:45) — “Ямар алных нь томуу вэ”
**Place:** livestock vehicle, approach to Naran. **POV:** Ganaa.
- On the radio: the official influenza message. Ganaa says the **protected line unchanged**: “Томуу гэнэ ээ? Хүрэлийг тэр чинь хазаад... идчихсэн шүү дээ. Ямар алных нь томуу вэ...”
- The driver asks what he said. Ganaa says it was nothing. Sheep block the road; his gaze settles on one animal's pulsing throat. He looks away when his face appears in the side glass. **No inner monologue. No self-slapping.**
- **Turn:** he recognizes what happened but not what is happening inside him. The official radio makes an emotional lie audible.

### 20 — 19:15–20:10 (0:55) — A choice at the hospital sign
**Place:** Naran medical lane / street on foot. **POV:** Ganaa.
- The livestock driver drops him in town (after a believable ~29-km route), offering to accompany him to a clinic; Ganaa declines, ashamed of his wound and terrified of being detained and missing his daughter's birthday.
- He actually walks toward the hospital direction sign, then stops when he sees tired families waiting and a guard asking people about injuries. He sees the wrapped mittens in his coat and turns toward home. He may try an ordinary pharmacy for a clean bandage; the sale is slow because the assistant is distracted by another alarmed caller.
- **Turn:** he makes the one consequential avoidant decision we can judge without blaming him for the outbreak. His unexplained delay moving through town is visible as disorientation and hesitation, not a jump cut through three hours.

### 21 — 20:10–21:05 (0:55) — Everybody is relieved; nobody feels safe
**Place:** apartment hall, living room and stair landing. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa.
- Ganaa enters to genuine relief, teasing and annoyance. There are enough guests left to make the party feel alive without a crowd physically impossible to animate. Tsetsegmaa immediately senses fever and notices the bloody wrist. Ganaa has a true answer about the truck being damaged, but withholds the terrifying reason.
- Sükhee is pleasantly drunk and wants to give Ganaa grief. Dulmaa tries to change the bandage. Ganaa pulls away, harder than he expects. A short silence ruptures the conviviality, then people talk over it because they want this to remain a party.
- **Turn:** the family starts to realize something is wrong; Ganaa discovers he may be dangerous.

### 22 — 21:05–22:00 (0:55) — A normal joke turns ugly
**Place:** living room / kitchen. **POV:** Ganaa and Tsetsegmaa.
- Sükhee jokes about Ganaa eating too fast. A bone cracks between Ganaa's teeth; he doesn't realize how hard he's biting. The relatives' laughter weakens. The mother-in-law, who normally solves problems with tea and bandages, sees that this isn't simple fatigue.
- Tsetsegmaa says quietly, with urgency, “Гараа үзүүлье.” Ganaa answers only “Дараа.” It is the same flaw that has structured his whole day. No long speech about becoming infected.
- **Turn:** his habitual avoidance stops being charming. **Echo:** he delayed Irina's errand, the clinic, and now his wife's help.

### 23 — 22:00–22:55 (0:55) — The one thing he manages to give her
**Place:** living room near birthday table. **POV:** Altan.
- Altan comes over in her paper crown. Ganaa kneels, somewhat unsteadily, and gives her the mittens. One is a little big. He guides her fingers into it instead of making a sentimental declaration. She asks him to sing the little tune. He starts too low and slightly off-key, like she knows he always does.
- Tsetsegmaa watches his hand with dread but doesn't pull Altan away abruptly: the girl believes she is safe in her father's presence, and the adults have not yet seen full transformation.
- **Turn:** for a moment he is present as a father. This is the last time the family is together in an ordinary configuration.

### 24 — 22:55–23:45 (0:50) — The sound from the next room
**Place:** living room into passage. **POV:** Ganaa's sensory experience, anchored objectively.
- The **visiting cousin's infant**, established earlier in the bedroom, wakes and cries because the party has become noisy. Ganaa's voice fails mid-song. Music keeps going for one beat before other people notice.
- Ganaa looks toward the bedroom, then down at Altan. His expression is neither a neat “I'm infected!” realization nor a generic monster face. He gently moves Altan's arm away from his own wrist; she thinks he is playing until he doesn't smile back.
- **Turn:** the audience cannot tell whether his movement toward the baby is involuntary appetite or whether he is recognizing danger and trying to resist it. Avoid monster-POV overlays or dramatic red eyes.

### 25 — 23:45–24:20 (0:35) — A tiny, nearly invisible act of protection
**Place:** apartment corridor / kitchen entrance. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa.
- Ganaa takes one step toward the crying infant, then forces himself sideways into the small kitchen, away from both children. He catches the door edge with his good hand. His injured wrist trembles.
- He looks once at Tsetsegmaa and Altan. He cannot finish a sentence. His face seems momentarily recognizable; then his breathing changes.
- Tsetsegmaa moves Altan toward the front door and gives Dulmaa a frightened look. The crowded hall, coats and furniture make those seconds slow without making everyone stupid.
- **Turn:** Ganaa's **action** suggests the father remains, but the audience has no guarantee.

### 26 — 24:20–25:00 (0:40) — ENDING B: THE DOOR
**Place:** kitchen door / narrow hallway. **POV:** Tsetsegmaa and Altan. **NO retrospective exposition.**
- Ganaa pulls the flimsy kitchen door closed from the inside and braces the handle. It has **no secure lock**. Behind it, the sound of a single dish sliding and his breath changing.
- Altan, wearing one new mitten, asks softly why her father isn't coming back. Tsetsegmaa is starting toward the apartment exit with her, but she can't look away from the door.
- Sükhee, a little drunk and misreading the action as a sick man collapsing, approaches the handle: **“Ганаа? Хөөе, юу болов?”** Tsetsegmaa warns him to wait, but the last action is already underway.
- The knob turns **slightly** under opposing hands. From inside comes one short human-sounding exhale that might be a plea; then a sharp, unnatural intake of breath.
- **CUT TO BLACK before the door opens.** No scream; no card declaring Ganaa dead; no misleading second fake cliffhanger. Hold two seconds of apartment-room sound, then silence.
- **Audience question:** Did he pull himself away to save Altan, and can that still work? **Episode 2 answers within its first minute.**

## What the pilot is actually about (under the horror)

- **Beginning:** Ganaa keeps people comfortable with jokes and vague promises.
- **Middle:** the same reflex makes him delay unpleasant facts: Dasha's condition, the urgent envelope, his injury.
- **End:** speech and jokes fail. The only meaningful thing he can still do is a small physical action: put a door between himself and his daughter. That action may be too late.
- **Tsetsegmaa's arc:** from tired familiarity (“he's late again”) → disbelief → instinctively choosing Altan's safety over the hope her husband can be rescued in that moment. She loves him without becoming passive.
- **Altan's arc:** no adult understanding of the infection. She waits, gets the promised mittens and song, then experiences the new terror of adults refusing to explain where her father went.
- **Hürel's arc:** a complete little friendship—debt, favors, teasing, food—that gets cut off mid-routine. The viewer remembers his life at the ruined café in Episode 8.
- **Naran's arc:** living, slightly shabby, functioning town → a town with something wrong beneath normal gossip → the start of distrust. It must not look abandoned yet.

## Protected acting and dialogue rules

- Dialogue should be **ordinary spoken Mongolian with subtext**; no “би өөрийгөө алдаж байна” speeches, no monster-desire narration, no forced “хөөрхий охин минь” monologues.
- Preserve organic profanity. Hürel can say “Битгий шаа” and Ganaa can swear when genuinely frightened. Erdene's later “Пизда” is a canon note for the series, not a line for this pilot.
- Don't make every minor character a stand-up comic. One natural remark, an unfinished joke, a glance and the silence afterward can be stronger than a crafted punchline.
- No voice-over inner monologue in EP01. Ganaa's behavior already reveals his dilemma.

## Avoid these four tempting but damaging shortcuts

1. **Don't put a monster in every town scene.** Normality needs contrast; the freight-yard cold open already buys us unease.
2. **Don't fake a thirty-minute emotional bond with a musical montage.** Let specific friction and gestures build relationships.
3. **Don't make Ganaa heroic by having him diagnose and explain himself.** The kitchen-door choice is more ambiguous and human.
4. **Don't withhold Altan's fate for half of Episode 2.** The cliff is resolved immediately. The next episode's larger mystery is why apparently bitten people are arriving from different places.

## Runtime & production feasibility

- **25:00 = 1,500 seconds = 36,000 frames at 24 fps.** This is three times the originally planned 8–10-minute pilot and must be budgeted accordingly.
- Aim for **six primary environments** with modular variants: freight yard/office, Naran apartment+landing, Naran market/clinic frontage, KAMAZ/border road, Hürel café+forecourt, livestock ride/Naran arrival. Distant town activity can be controlled background assets.
- Crowd scale: 12–18 late-evening party actors visible in clusters, not 40 simultaneously full-rigged; earlier visitors implied through coat piles, plates and sound.
- Animatic test first for **(a)** 03:20–05:50 town/family character scenes, **(b)** 14:25–18:30 café escalation, and **(c)** 21:05–25:00 birthday/door ending. If any section drags in a silent table read, tighten it *before* final environmental asset work.
- Use clean sound motifs and shot composition to make routine scenes engaging without endless walking cycles or elaborate NPC animation.

## Approval gates before screenplay lock

1. Verify real-world route/weather/period props; lock **fictional** road travel separately from historical reference.
2. Set exact diegetic clock for departure, border, Dasha pickup, wrist bite, Naran arrival, birthday and first major loss of control. Simulate in a 1-row-per-event spreadsheet.
3. Block apartment floorplan. Door must be reachable, bracable from kitchen and openable by an outsider. Bedroom infant audible from birthday table; entry/stair escape genuinely usable but crowded.
4. Block café forecourt collision and ignition source. Protect KAMAZ satchel in the same geometry used for Episode 8.
5. Record scratch dialogue with native Mongolian performers; don't assign emotional labels to every line.
6. Run a blind screening of the 25-minute animatic: ask (a) who they care about; (b) why Ganaa didn't go to hospital; (c) what happened with the kitchen door; (d) whether they want Episode 2. Don't explain first.

**Not canon yet:** exact beat lengths; names of Nyamka/Bataa/Togoo; the clinic frontage sequence; whether Ganaa makes the door close fully; the detailed stunt motion after Episode 2 opens. All are deliberate v4.0 proposals subject to blockout and timed read-through.
