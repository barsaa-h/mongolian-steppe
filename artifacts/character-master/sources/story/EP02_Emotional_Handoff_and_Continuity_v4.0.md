# ХАНИАД — EP01 → EP02: THE DOOR AND ITS CONSEQUENCES
## Episode 2 «ХОЁР ХААЛГА» (TWO DOORS) — v4.0 bridge treatment

**Status:** New proposal, not the locked Episode 2 script. Designed to replace the previous opening while preserving v3.0's key events: Tsetsegmaa and Altan survive, Sükhee is bitten, Dr. Oyun triages patients, Narantsetseg gives birth to Naran, a maternity breach creates an impossible door decision, and the cover-up continues into Episode 3.

## Why the ending B works only with a fast reveal

The Episode 1 cliff does not ask “Did Altan die?” for 20 minutes. It asks whether Ganaa's recognizable act of pulling away from her will work. **Within the first 60–90 seconds of Episode 2, viewers learn it didn't work completely—but it bought her life.** The consequence transfers the episode's central emotional investment from father to mother and daughter, then into the doctor's decisions.

## Episode 2 opening — continuous action, no replay montage

### 00:00–00:12 — Kitchen door, the very next seconds
- Start on the same apartment corridor. The handle rattles; Ganaa braces from inside. Sükhee says something impatient, not heroic, and begins opening it because he believes Ganaa is ill and about to collapse.
- Tsetsegmaa snaps: **“Сүхээ, наад хаалгыг битгий нээ!”** He is already pulling. **No long gap** between installments; the viewer gets the promised continuation.

### 00:12–00:45 — One mistake and a child saved
- The door opens a crack. Ganaa lunges and bites Sükhee's **upper arm** during the struggle (lock exact bite location and depth for onset timing). Sükhee is not immediately transformed. Ganaa does **not** instantly become invincible; the force is close-quarters and confused.
- Tsetsegmaa scoops Altan away. Dulmaa opens the front door toward the stair landing, knocks a shoe rack aside, and pulls them through. One guest falls while moving toward the bedroom infant; the infant's parent carries the child out through the same corridor after the first crowd clears. Document which people leave and which remain.
- Sükhee's reflex is to push the kitchen door shut behind him; another guest wedges an ordinary wooden stool against it. The stool is a temporary obstacle, not a magical zombie barrier.

### 00:45–01:25 — A child thinks the problem is punishment
- On the landing Altan asks, holding her one mitten, why her father is angry. Tsetsegmaa doesn't have a safe explanation. She says **“Аав чинь өвдчихсөн байна. Ээжтэйгээ явъя.”** (sample; not locked).
- From inside, something hits the kitchen door. We do **not** show the father's death or imply he later makes secret lucid speeches. He is left in the apartment. A neighbor calls for the duty ambulance/police through the building's landline or a nearby shop.
- **Emotional shift:** Ganaa's final bracing of the door gave Altan time to escape. His good intention matters; it is not a cure and it cannot undo the harm.

### 01:25–03:15 — The cost becomes ordinary logistics
- Cold night exterior: no miraculous fleet of ambulances. The injured are split between an available town vehicle and a neighbor's older car. Sükhee keeps saying he is fine while bleeding onto someone's birthday paper napkin. His embarrassed joking continues too long and makes the seriousness worse.
- Tsetsegmaa demands that someone take Altan somewhere safe; no one knows where safe is. Dulmaa refuses to leave the girl alone. A person tries to telephone the hospital and waits on a busy line. The outbreak is beginning, not already fully global.
- **Important:** injured people go to the hospital; Ganaa stays behind the barricaded kitchen door when the household flees. Responders later isolate the apartment instead of using him as a roaming monster that conveniently appears wherever the plot needs him.

### 03:15–05:20 — Hospital: Oyun inherits their story
- At the emergency desk, confetti, single mitten, frantic fragments of eyewitness accounts. **Oyun asks practical questions**: which injured people were bitten, where, at what time? Tsetsegmaa can't give clean answers because she never saw the whole attack.
- Oyun initially treats Sükhee and the other guests as injury patients; no one yet knows a reliable infection clock. She sees a more severe patient turn while staff perform a sensible intervention. Her focus changes from routine triage to containment.
- Tsetsegmaa tries to leave with Altan after an examination. She is held by a changing quarantine order, laying groundwork for later anger toward officials. Oyun's sympathy is not a speech; she gives Altan a clean blanket while continuing to direct triage.

### 05:20 onward — Episode 2's independent dramatic problem
- Bring in **Narantsetseg**, who is already in labor with clinical signs of fetal distress. She came to the hospital before the Ganaa survivors; she is not coincidentally in the birthday party.
- Bolor and Oyun must manage injured civilians and the maternity corridor. The breach and Bolor's bite happen because staff physically attempt to move patients through inadequate space; they aren't teleporting enemies.
- Narantsetseg saves a baby at the cost of a bite; a team attempts a medically plausible time-compressed urgent delivery with visible personnel/equipment. Baby Naran survives.
- An infected breach separates mothers with their children on one side from Oyun and Naran near a maintenance exit. **Oyun's door dilemma is a parallel to Ganaa's door**: both close a door to protect a child, but Oyun must live with that decision. Do not imply the hospital gate mechanically locks from the wrong side unless the floorplan visibly supports it.
- Tsetsegmaa, Altan and Dulmaa remain on the **town-side route**, eventually directed toward the temporary public shelter and later westbound civilian convoy (Ep4–5). They are **not** secretly in the sealed maternity ward.

## A thematic echo to make the season feel designed, not plotted

**Episode 1:** The door is Ganaa's last attempted act of care. He cannot ask for help clearly, and his neighbor mistakes his intention. 

**Episode 2:** The door is Oyun's first conscious, irrevocable choice. She understands more, yet cannot rescue everyone. 

**Episode 3:** The governor closes a third door—information. He can explain his decision in polite bureaucratic language, but it puts others at risk. 

The series' emerging motif is **what people shut out to protect the people beside them**. Use it lightly: behavior and spaces, not title-card speeches.

## Plot-hole prevention / questions to answer before locking Ep2

1. **When exactly does Sükhee get bitten and when do his symptoms start?** The bite is not an instant transformation. Another patient can turn quickly from a head/torso bite; don't accidentally assign Sükhee a neck-turn clock while showing an arm wound.
2. **Where is Ganaa's body when responders arrive?** Inside the apartment at the last verified time. If he later escapes, show how, when, and why—not a silent retcon. Prefer keeping his ultimate fate onscreen established by the response team in EP02 or EP03 rather than inventing a serial-killer chase arc.
3. **Where is the visiting infant and parent?** Draw a path from bedroom to apartment door and record who carries the child. Don't sacrifice a baby off-screen just for shock.
4. **Why can Tsetsegmaa and Altan leave while Oyun cannot rescue everyone?** They are in different hospital zones/areas at different times. The layout and quarantine boundaries must explain this visually.
5. **Does the public already know something is wrong?** Some unrelated suspicious cases may have preceded Ganaa. The physician's uncertainty is genuine; the governor's earlier unverified warning is a separate, politically important fact.
6. **Why does Oyun not just open a second exit?** Physical blocking study: one hallway overrun, maintenance stair accessible, second door impossible to traverse in time with babies and patients. No invisible walls.
7. **What do children understand?** Altan is three; her speech is short, concrete, and repetitive, not adult insight disguised as a child.
8. **Do the episodes have enough time?** A 25-minute pilot followed by a 9-minute Episode 2 would rush the maternal consequences. Reconsider Episode 2's runtime after a timed act breakdown, rather than retaining the obsolete 8–10-minute mandate automatically.

## Recommended Episode 2 runtime decision (proposal)

Target **18–22 minutes** for Episode 2 *if* we keep the birthday escape, hospital triage, Narantsetseg's labor, maternity breach, and Oyun's choice in one episode. Otherwise split the hospital aftermath across two episodes and adjust the 10-episode season architecture. Do not compress an emergency C-section and a complete hospital collapse into three on-screen minutes simply to hit a legacy length target.

## Cliffhanger integrity test

Ask five unfamiliar viewers after EP01: “What do you think Ganaa was trying to do when he closed the kitchen door?” Mixed answers are good. Ask after the first **minute** of EP02: “Do you know whether Altan survived the initial attack?” Everyone should say **yes**. Ask at the EP02 midpoint: “What must Oyun decide?” The answer should not be “I don't know who this doctor is.”
