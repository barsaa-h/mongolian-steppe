# CH12 — Naran wardrobe library specification

**Status: draft; all outfits, colours and garment designs below are artistic choices pending Mongolian review.** This is a production specification, not an inventory of finished assets or a claim of historical verification. No web research was used. Script facts and proposed continuity are distinguished below. The world is a fictional northern aimag centre in late November 1998; repaired clothing and cared-for clothing coexist. Indoor party clothes need not be outdoor winter layers.

## Period working hypotheses

Every historical claim in this section is **common knowledge, to verify** with the creator; examples are possibilities, not a uniform imposed on a group.

- Drivers and labourers: padded work jackets, heavy trousers, work boots and caps alongside older Soviet-style workwear; layers and repairs reflect occupation and means.
- Market and kiosk traders: practical warm coats, knitted or fur hats, scarves and boots; a long working day outdoors calls for layers.
- Office and civil workers: wool coats, suits or skirts with warm hosiery, shirts and sweaters; Soviet-era and post-Soviet Western styles could coexist.
- Pensioners: older coats, fur or knitted hats, and everyday padded deels may coexist; older and rural people are plausible frequent deel wearers, without implying all elders dress alike.
- Women at home: dresses, skirts or trousers with cardigans and aprons; some women wear everyday deels. A deel is ordinary clothing for some people, not a ceremonial costume for everyone.
- Schoolchildren and toddlers: warm coats, hats, mittens and boots outdoors; school clothes may include dark separates and light shirts, but the local uniform and 1998 rules need checking. Toddlers need appropriately bulky warm layers.
- Medical staff: white coats and caps are plausible inside, with ordinary winter outerwear for travel; local uniform details need checking.
- Police and officials: service uniforms and winter coats are plausible; exact 1998 cut, insignia and local practice need checking before any recognizable badge is made.
- Russian visitors and workers: practical Soviet/post-Soviet coats, knitwear, trousers and fur hats could overlap with Mongolian townspeople's clothing; nationality alone does not determine an outfit.

**Artistic choice:** subdued blue, brown, green and burgundy families, with varied silhouettes, repairs and a few brighter party accents. Avoid modern logos and T-shirts; don't imply that every household is poor or dressed in a deel.

## Build and integration contract

The existing `build_character.py` resolves each `clothing.asset` and `clothing.shoes` to `assets/sources/makehuman-core/clothes/<asset>/<asset>.mhclo` and calls `HumanService.add_mhclo_asset` on the body, reading the referenced material; the shared builder supplies the rig. `build_actor.py` applies appearance, recolours materials and adds some procedural costume pieces weighted to the shared rig. DNA currently has exactly `asset`, `shoes`, `wardrobe`, `top_color`, `bottom_color`, `accent_color`; it does **not** already accept the multiple catalogue IDs above. These rows are a design mapping, not valid DNA. Multi-layer selection, new paths, individual state controls and custom accessories need a later integration brief and compatible validation/library changes; do not edit sources or pretend a custom mesh is already loaded by the builder.

For reusable body-following garments, author MakeHuman/MPFB `.mhclo` proxy packages with reference mesh, vertex-to-base mapping, deletion mask where appropriate, material and textures; fit against the complete authoring body, then verify transferred weights and deformation on the shared 137-bone rig. For bulky coats, open hems, crown and hand mittens, use custom rigged meshes or separate prop meshes when proxy mapping cannot preserve shape; supply source `.blend`, weights, attachments and export-ready materials. Mittens need individually attachable left/right child-hand versions and a separate pocket/table prop; fit the actual Base C when available. Do not destroy underlying authoring topology: use reversible visibility/masks, and test layered intersections. Test adult male/female and child fits only where specified; the unfinished child base is a dependency, not a completed fit.

Reuse a community asset only after inspecting its per-asset licence and provenance: CC0 is suitable without attribution; CC-BY requires recorded creator, source, licence/version, changes and attribution in distribution. Verify the exact licence and any dependencies before import; do not assume every MakeHuman community upload is CC0 or CC-BY. No named external asset has been vetted here. If suitable licensed assets are unavailable, model each catalogue shape; Ganaa's tearable cuff, Altan's mittens and crown, locally specific deel/gutal, and uniform details need bespoke work or explicit adaptation. Keep handmade sources outside generated output folders and original `sources/` untouched.

**Budget targets for the later jobs, not measured results:** T2 complete dressed crowd actor ≤5,000 triangles, 256–512 px textures total per atlas; reserve ≤1,500 triangles for the complete visible clothing set, including footwear and headwear, and reduce further if body/hair exceed the balance. T1 clothing set target ≤6,000 triangles and 1K textures. T0 hero clothing set target ≤20,000 triangles and 2K–4K textures; spend detail only on visible inserts. These are proposed caps, not guarantees of final performance; record actual counts including accessories and body. Use atlas/material sharing and preserve silhouette.

## Questions for Mongolian review

1. In a northern aimag centre in 1998, how common were everyday deels among older people and women, indoors and outdoors?
2. Which local winter hat, boot, fabric and coat cuts or names here feel wrong for late November?
3. What would an ordinary family's birthday best clothes and a three-year-old's winter layers look like in that town?
4. What school uniform, if any, was actually worn locally in 1998?
5. What did local medical and police uniforms, caps and insignia look like, and were they worn outside work?
6. Are the proposed patched workwear, Russian visitor outfits and muted palette plausible across different incomes, or too uniform?
