# BLENDER CHARACTER REMASTER — ORIGINAL SILENT HILL 2 (2001) STYLE

## PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Improve my existing 3D character to match the visual quality and artistic language of **Silent Hill 2 (2001), the original PlayStation 2 game by Team Silent**.

DO NOT USE THE 2024 REMAKE AS A REFERENCE.

My character currently looks too simplified, blocky, and almost like a toy. I want him to feel like an actual human character from an early-2000s psychological horror game.

**This is NOT a photorealism project.**

The target is early-PS2 cinematic character art: low-poly human anatomy, carefully painted textures, restrained detail, simplified yet recognizable facial features, and subtle emotional animation.

Keep the original character's identity, proportions where appropriate, hairstyle, green winter jacket, white collar, trousers, and boots.

Do not make him look like James Sunderland. Adopt the artistic techniques, not the character's identity.

---

## 1. STRICT ART STYLE RULES

### Target
- Original Silent Hill 2 (2001) PS2-era character visuals.
- Human-like but visibly game-stylized.
- Slightly angular facial structure.
- Simplified organic shapes.
- Low-to-moderate polygon count.
- Painted diffuse textures.
- Subtle lighting-based facial depth.
- Serious psychological-horror atmosphere.
- Believable anatomy without high-frequency detail.

### Absolutely avoid
- Photorealistic facial scans.
- MetaHuman-style faces.
- Detailed skin pores.
- Realistic individual hair strands.
- Modern hyper-detailed PBR character rendering.
- Excessive mesh subdivision.
- Pixar or Disney proportions.
- Oversized cartoon eyes.
- LEGO-like or Roblox-like anatomy.
- Overly smooth plastic skin.
- Random blocky textures covering every surface.

The character should look as though he belongs in an exceptionally well-directed PS2 horror game, rendered clearly at 720p.

---

## 2. FACE — MOST IMPORTANT

The current face lacks depth and emotional range.

Improve the face with the following limited but important changes.

### Facial structure
- Preserve the character's recognizable features.
- Improve the shape of the nose, cheekbones, jawline, chin, forehead and ears.
- Make the nose protrude convincingly from the skull.
- Add subtle nostril geometry or painted nostril shadows.
- Improve eye socket depth.
- Correct the connection between face and neck.
- Maintain the angular, slightly imperfect low-poly silhouette.

**Do not increase facial geometry merely to make it smoother.**

### Eyes
Eyes are the highest-priority feature.

- Create properly positioned eyeballs or simplified eye surfaces.
- Add clear eyelid geometry.
- Improve iris and pupil textures.
- Give the eyes believable depth.
- Add subtle dark areas around the eyelids.
- Improve eyebrow shapes.
- Allow natural blinking, squinting and eye widening.

The eyes should convey fear, sadness, exhaustion and anger without cartoon exaggeration.

### Mouth
- Improve upper and lower lip shapes.
- Add a small but functional mouth opening.
- Create simplified inner-mouth geometry.
- Support jaw movement.
- Support subtle changes in mouth corners.
- Avoid excessively thick, smooth or realistic lips.

The mouth must support serious dialogue and emotional performances.

### Facial textures
Paint subtle color transitions and shadows into the face texture.

Prioritize recognizable:
- Eyelids and under-eye shadows.
- Nasal bridge and nostrils.
- Lip separation.
- Cheek structure.
- Jaw definition.
- Brow shadows.

Avoid photographic skin textures.

The texture should resemble carefully painted early-2000s game character art.

---

## 3. FACIAL ANIMATION — EMOTION WITHOUT EXCESSIVE REALISM

Implement a lightweight facial animation system.

Prefer simple facial bones and/or a small number of carefully designed shape keys.

Required expressions:

1. Neutral.
2. Sadness.
3. Fear.
4. Anger.
5. Shock.
6. Pain.
7. Exhaustion.
8. Subtle smile.
9. Crying or grief.
10. Vacant zombie expression.

Required movements:
- Blink.
- Eyebrow raise and lower.
- Eyelid widening and narrowing.
- Eye direction.
- Jaw open and close.
- Mouth corner movement.
- Lip compression.
- Basic speech mouth shapes.

Prioritize approximately 12–20 useful expression controls, expanding only when actual animation tests justify it.

The emotional performance should be subtle, sometimes slightly stiff, and consistent with original Silent Hill 2's style.

Do not create theatrical cartoon expressions.

For emotional scenes, combine facial animation with head motion, breathing, posture and careful lighting.

The character should communicate sadness even through very small movements.

---

## 4. HAIR — SIMPLE BUT BELIEVABLE

The current hair looks like a solid black helmet.

Keep short stylized polygon hair, but improve its construction.

- Establish a believable hairline.
- Correct the side and back silhouette.
- Separate the hair into a small number of intentional directional clumps.
- Use dark brown-black color variation.
- Paint subtle strand direction into the hair texture.
- Introduce restrained highlights.
- Keep the mesh angular.

DO NOT create realistic individual hair strands or use an expensive particle hairstyle.

The hair should resemble the geometry and painted-hair techniques seen on original Silent Hill 2 characters.

---

## 5. BODY PROPORTIONS

The body currently feels like a toy because of its stiff silhouette and exaggerated geometric shapes.

Improve:
- Shoulder width and natural slope.
- Head-to-body proportions.
- Neck length and thickness.
- Torso shape.
- Pelvis proportions.
- Arm length.
- Hand size.
- Leg anatomy.
- Knee position.
- Foot size.

Preserve the stylized low-poly design.

Do not add detailed muscles or photorealistic anatomy.

The character should resemble an ordinary adult male in winter clothing, not an action figure.

Pay particular attention to the side profile and how the jacket fits around the torso.

---

## 6. SKIN COLORS AND TEXTURES

The current skin appears muddy and excessively orange.

Create a cleaner, natural complexion while preserving the character's identity.

Use:
- Warm-neutral base skin tone.
- Slight cheek and nose color variation.
- Restrained shading around eyes.
- Subtle lip color.
- Painted shadows around the jaw.
- Low-frequency color variation.

Use simple diffuse textures supported by restrained material shading.

Suggested initial head texture: 512×512 or 1024×1024.

Use the higher option when expressions genuinely benefit from it, not merely because higher resolution is available.

Avoid overly sharp pores, photographic skin maps, and glossy or waxy materials.

---

## 7. CLOTHING IMPROVEMENT

Keep the original outfit recognizable.

### Green jacket
- Correct the jacket's boxy silhouette.
- Improve its fit around shoulders and waist.
- Add naturally placed polygonal folds.
- Refine pockets and zipper.
- Maintain slightly angular PS2-style geometry.
- Use painted fabric detail instead of excessive mesh complexity.

### White collar
The collar currently looks like a collection of stones.

Replace excessive white lumps with simplified fleece shapes and better painted fabric texture.

### Trousers
Improve upper-leg anatomy and knee placement. Add restrained clothing folds.

### Boots
Maintain simple geometry. Improve proportions and add only the details visible during animation.

Do not over-detail the clothing beyond the face.

---

## 8. SUGGESTED TECHNICAL BUDGET

These are proposed budgets for an enhanced PS2-inspired character, NOT claims about original Silent Hill 2 model specifications.

- Full character: initially target approximately 6,000–12,000 triangles.
- Spend geometry on silhouette, face and deformation.
- Use 512–1024 px facial textures.
- Use 512–1024 px textures for most clothing and hair assets, increasing selectively where required.
- Keep materials simple and easy to light.
- Use smooth shading selectively.
- Preserve intentionally faceted areas.
- Avoid unnecessary modifiers and subdivisions.

A few extra triangles are acceptable if they visibly improve close-up acting.

The goal is correct visual style, not the lowest possible triangle count.

---

## 9. ZOMBIE AND HORROR COMPATIBILITY

The upgraded characters must support horror animation.

Prepare reusable systems for:
- Zombie skin variations.
- Blood and dirt textures.
- Torn clothing.
- Facial injuries.
- Damaged body-part variants.
- Separatable body components.
- Stiff or distorted zombie animation.

These effects should follow the same early-PS2 graphical style.

Use painted wounds, controlled polygon geometry, and modular meshes.

Do not introduce modern photorealistic gore assets into an otherwise retro character.

Keep clean originals intact.

---

## 10. RIGGING

Create an animation-friendly body and facial rig.

Support:
- Walking and running.
- Falling.
- Crawling.
- Grabbing.
- Basic combat.
- Shivering.
- Breathing.
- Head movements.
- Expressive eyes and mouth.
- Zombie movement.
- Controlled body-part separation using prepared variants.

Ensure deformation is believable despite the intentionally simple geometry.

Preserve existing animation compatibility wherever possible.

---

## 11. RENDERING AND ART DIRECTION

Test the character using lighting inspired by the original Silent Hill 2:

- Cold, desaturated exterior lighting.
- Harsh overhead interior lighting.
- Dark side-lit facial close-ups.
- Restrained warm lighting.
- Heavy shadows.
- Subtle environmental atmosphere.

However, also render neutral studio previews to reveal modeling defects.

Do not rely on darkness or fog to conceal poor geometry.

Use 1280×720 as the principal delivery test resolution.

Avoid deliberately blurring every texture to simulate old hardware. Preserve the original art style while making it legible.

# FINAL REQUIREMENT

When I see the completed character, I should think:

**"This looks like a character that could have appeared in Silent Hill 2 in 2001, except the facial expressions are slightly better and the model is cleaner."**

I should NOT think:

"This looks like a realistic modern game character."

And I should NOT think:

"This looks like a low-budget LEGO animation."

Prioritize strong stylized anatomy, painted facial detail, subtle emotional animation, and an authentically unsettling early-PS2 atmosphere.

**Better artistic modeling, not more realism.**
