# Russian lines — stress, pronunciation, Mongolian gloss

There are **44 Russian cues** in EP01. The text is fixed; this sheet only helps
you say it. The stress mark is the acute accent on the stressed vowel
(а́, е́, и́, о́, у́, ы́, я́). `ё` is always stressed, so it is not marked twice.

Latin is a reading guide, not a transliteration standard: **CAPITALS** mark the
stressed syllable, `'` marks a soft consonant, and `kh` is the hard `х`.

## Who is native and who is not

- **Irina** — 54, Russian epidemiologist, **native Russian**. Full vowel
  reduction, clear soft signs, no theatre. She is the most formally correct
  speaker in the episode.
- **Dasha** — 24, Russian, **native Russian**. Her Russian is correct but
  clipped by injury; she does not finish every ending.
- **Yard guard, Pasha, border officer, dispatcher** — Russian, native. They are
  ordinary working Russians in 1998, not accents from a spy film.
- **Ganaa** — Mongolian, speaks **Russian with a Mongolian accent**. That is
  correct and should stay. Do not "improve" his Russian; do not reduce his
  vowels the way Irina does.
- **Hürel** — one Russian line («Чай? Садись, садись.») in a simple, friendly
  Mongolian accent. That is also correct.

## Tricky sounds

### ы

Not English "ee" and not "i". Pull the tongue back, keep the lips unrounded,
and make a dark, tense vowel — close to Turkish `ı` or the vowel in Scottish
"gib". In the Latin column it is written `y`.

- `Слышь` = slysh (one syllable, not "sleesh").
- `Выходи` = vy-ha-DEE.
- `Быстро` = BY-stra.

### щ

A long, soft `shch`, not `sh` and not `s-ch` with a pause. Start the `sh` with
the tongue high and spread, then release into a soft `ch`.

- `сообщение` = so-ob-SHCHE-ni-ye.
- `обещал` = a-be-SHCHAL.

### Soft sign ь

`ь` has no sound of its own. It palatalises the consonant before it: the middle
of the tongue rises toward the hard palate. Do not pronounce a separate `y`.

- `больница` = bal'-NEE-tsa (light `l`, then `n`).
- `ремeнь` = re-MYEN' (the final `n` is soft).
- `садись` = sa-DEES' (soft final `s`).
- `только` = TOL'-ka (soft `l`).

### Other traps

- **ё is always stressed**: `доживёт` = da-zhee-VYOT, `ещё` = ye-SHCHO.
- **Unstressed о becomes а** in native Russian: `хорошо` = ha-ra-SHO,
  `понял` is closer to PA-nyal. Do this for Irina, Dasha and the Russian men;
  do **not** overdo it for Ganaa.
- **Final voiced consonants devoice**: `раз` = ras, `друг` = druk.
- **что** = shto, not "chto". **сегодня** = se-VOD-nya.
- **милиция**, not «полиция» — the episode is set in 1998.
- **Names:** `Нара́н` (town), `Га́на` (Russian vocative of Ganaa), `Хүрэл`
  stays Mongolian even inside a Russian sentence.
- **Ganaa's accent:** keep Mongolian vowel clarity and consonant hardness. His
  Russian is fluent but not native; that is a script fact about the character,
  not a mistake to fix.
- **Irina and Dasha:** native Russian. Do not put a "foreign" accent on them,
  and do not make Irina sound like a villain. Her precision is the point.

## Recording these lines

- Do not read the Latin column while recording. Read the Russian column; use
  Latin only to check a word before the take.
- The stress mark is a guide to the dictionary stress, not an instruction to
  hit the vowel harder. Russian stress is mostly length and clarity, not volume.
- Keep the punctuation. The ellipses in `Снова. Примерно три минуты... и опять
  к теплу.` are pauses in a written note, not dramatic beats.
- Profanity stays in Mongolian for Ganaa; the Russian lines here have no
  invented swearing, so do not add any.
- If a Russian word keeps coming out with the wrong stress, record it alone
  three times, then put it back into the line. Do not slow the whole line down.
